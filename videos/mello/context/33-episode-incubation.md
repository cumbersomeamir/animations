## Research (sources checked 2026-11-21)
1. **Baird, B., Smallwood, J., Mrazek, M. D., Kam, J. W. Y., Franklin, M. S., & Schooler, J. W. (2012).** Inspired by distraction: Mind wandering facilitates creative incubation. *Psychological Science, 23*(10), 1117–1122. https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797612446024 (PMID 22941876; abstract read; full text closed access)
   - "We used an incubation paradigm to assess whether performance on validated creativity problems (the Unusual Uses Task, or UUT) can be facilitated by engaging in either a demanding task or an undemanding task that maximizes mind wandering."
   - "Compared with engaging in a demanding task, rest, or no break, engaging in an undemanding task during an incubation period led to substantial improvements in performance on previously encountered problems."
   - "the context that improved performance after the incubation period was associated with higher levels of mind wandering but not with a greater number of explicitly directed thoughts about the UUT."
   - Design details from Teng & Lien 2022 (below, open access): the low-load task was a 0-back task and the high-load task a 1-back task; performance was measured "upon a second attempt"; the low-load group's self-rated mind wandering was higher than the high-load group's, "although comparably with the resting group".
2. **Sio, U. N., & Ormerod, T. C. (2009).** Does incubation enhance problem solving? A meta-analytic review. *Psychological Bulletin, 135*(1), 94–120. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0014212 (PMID 19210055; abstract)
   - "The authors identified a positive incubation effect, with divergent thinking tasks benefiting more than linguistic and visual insight tasks from incubation."
   - "filling an incubation period with high cognitive demand tasks gave a smaller incubation effect. Surprisingly, low cognitive demand tasks yielded a stronger incubation effect than did rest during an incubation period when solving linguistic insight problems."
   - "the conditions under which incubation can be used as a practical technique for enhancing problem solving must be designed with care."
3. **Limits (follow-ups):**
   - **Murray, S., Liang, N., Brosowsky, N. P., & Seli, P. (2021; issue 2024).** What are the benefits of mind wandering to creativity? *Psychology of Aesthetics, Creativity, and the Arts.* https://doi.org/10.1037/aca0000420 (abstract via Semantic Scholar)
     - "we attempted to conceptually replicate results of a highly cited laboratory-based experiment supporting this finding. However, across two studies (N = 443), we found no evidence for the claim that mind wandering during a creative-incubation interval facilitates a form of creativity associated with divergent thinking."
     - Teng & Lien 2022 summarise it: "no difference in the improvement of UUT performance upon a second attempt was found between the low- and high-load groups".
   - **Teng, S.-C., & Lien, Y.-W. (2022).** Propensity or diversity? Investigating how mind wandering influences the incubation effect of creativity. *PLoS One, 17*(4), e0267187. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0267187 (PMID 35486594; PMC9053813, full text read)
     - "the mind wandering-prone group (N = 29) did not outperform the control group".
     - The review section adds: Yamaoka & Yukawa found no group difference, and Smeekens & Kane found no correlation between mind wandering and UUT improvement.
   - **McDaniel, C., Habibi, A., & Kaplan, J. (2025).** Mind wandering during creative incubation predicts increases in creative performance in a writing task. *Scientific Reports, 15*, 24629. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-09736-y (PMID 40634381; abstract)
     - "Results showed no significant differences in creative improvement (pre- to post-incubation) across incubation tasks" (0-back, 2-back, meditation, no break).
     - However, "greater mind wandering during incubation predicted greater within-subject creative improvement … for participants who received the same prompt after the break".
   - The literature on the *easy-task edge* is therefore mixed, and mostly not replicated in group comparisons. Incubation in general has meta-analytic support (Sio & Ormerod 2009).

How the script uses them (measured / reported / advice / illustrative):

- **Meta-analysis (Sio & Ormerod 2009), hedged:** "A stuck problem can loosen when you step away." ("can": a positive average effect, larger for divergent tasks and for some conditions).
- **Measured (Baird 2012):**

- **Not claimed:** genius, guaranteed solutions, that mind wandering is the proven cause, or any effect size.
