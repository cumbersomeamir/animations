## Research (sources checked 2026-11-15; PubMed abstracts)
1. **Sturm, V. E., Datta, S., Roy, A. R. K., Sible, I. J., Kosik, E. L., Veziris, C. R., Chow, T. E., Morris, N. A., Neuhaus, J., Kramer, J. H., Miller, B. L., Holley, S. R., & Keltner, D. (2022).** Big smile, small self: Awe walks promote prosocial positive emotions in older adults. *Emotion, 22*(5), 1044–1058. https://doi.org/10.1037/emo0000876 (PMID 32955293)
   - "Sixty participants took weekly 15-min outdoor walks for 8 weeks; participants were randomly assigned to an awe walk group … or to a control walk group."
   - Awe walkers "exhibited an increasingly 'small self' in their photographs over time … displayed increasing smile intensity".
   - Outside the walks they "reported greater increases in daily prosocial positive emotions and greater decreases in daily distress over time".
   - "Postintervention anxiety, depression, and life satisfaction did not change from baseline in either group."
   - Awe is defined as "a positive emotion elicited when in the presence of vast things not immediately understood".
2. **Piff, P. K., Dietze, P., Feinberg, M., Stancato, D. M., & Keltner, D. (2015).** Awe, the small self, and prosocial behavior. *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 108*(6), 883–899. https://doi.org/10.1037/pspi0000018 (PMID 25984788)
   - Five studies (N = 2,078). Inductions of awe increased prosociality. Standing in a grove of towering trees enhanced helping and decreased entitlement. Effects were explained "in part, by feelings of a small self".
   - Used as background for the "small self" and the grove setting, not spoken.

How the script uses them (measured / definition / illustrative):
- **Definition (Sturm 2022):** "It's called awe: the feeling near something vast, like a night sky."
- **Measured (Sturm 2022, one randomized study of 60 healthy older adults):** "In one study, older adults took weekly fifteen-minute walks for eight weeks. Those looking for awe took selfies with more scenery, less self, and bigger smiles. They also reported less daily distress."
  - "Less daily distress" means a greater decrease in daily distress than the control walkers.
  - Anxiety, depression and life satisfaction did not change; the script makes no claim about them.
- **Advice:** "So on your next walk, find one vast thing, a sky, a tree, a skyline, and pause for it." An application, with no promised outcome.
- **Illustrative:** the four selfies (labelled weeks 1/3/5/8: the self shrinks, the scenery and the smile grow) and the "daily distress" bar (direction only). The scene, moon and grove are staging.
