## Research (sources checked 2026-11-10)
1. **Redelmeier, D. A., & Kahneman, D. (1996).** Patients' memories of painful medical treatments: Real-time and retrospective evaluations of two minimally invasive procedures. *Pain, 66*(1), 3–8. https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3959(96)02994-6 (PubMed abstract, PMID 8857625)
   - "Patients' judgments of total pain were strongly correlated with the peak intensity of pain … and with the intensity of pain recorded during the last 3 min of the procedure."
   - "Despite substantial variation in the duration of the experience, lengthy procedures were not remembered as particularly aversive."
2. **Fredrickson, B. L., & Kahneman, D. (1993).** Duration neglect in retrospective evaluations of affective episodes. *JPSP, 65*(1), 45–55. https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.65.1.45 (PubMed abstract, PMID 8355141)
   - "Retrospective evaluations appear to be determined by a weighted average of 'snapshots' of the actual affective experience, as if duration did not matter."
3. **Redelmeier, D. A., Katz, J., & Kahneman, D. (2003).** Memories of colonoscopy: A randomized trial. *Pain, 104*(1–2), 187–194. https://doi.org/10.1016/S0304-3959(03)00003-4 (PubMed abstract, PMID 12855328)
   - n = 682, randomised; half had a short, gentler interval added at the end.
   - Those patients "experienced the final moments as less painful … rated the entire experience as less unpleasant (4.4 vs. 4.9 on a 10 cm visual analogue scale, P=0.006)".
4. Kahneman, Fredrickson, Schreiber & Redelmeier (1993, *Psychological Science*), the cold-water study, was **not used**: its abstract is withheld by the publisher, so its details could not be checked.

How the script uses them:
- "we tend to remember the most intense moment and the ending" is the peak and final-minutes finding (1996), with "tend to" for a tendency.
- "In a medical study, gentler final minutes made patients remember the whole procedure as less unpleasant" is the 2003 randomised trial (rated less unpleasant). No numbers are spoken.
- "It won't decide every memory, but endings carry weight" is the honest scope: an association and one trial, not a rule for every memory.
- "end your workday by finishing one small, meaningful task" is an application of the end effect to one's own day. It is advice, not a claimed outcome.
- The cards are schematic. The day-out curve and the "in memory" bars show direction only.
