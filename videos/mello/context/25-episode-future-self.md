## Research (sources checked 2026-11-15; PubMed abstracts)
1. **Pronin, E., Olivola, C. Y., & Kennedy, K. A. (2008).** Doing unto future selves as you would do unto others: Psychological distance and decision making. *Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin, 34*(2), 224–236. https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167207310023 (PMID 18156588)
   - "Four experiments showed that the decisions people make for future selves and other people are similar to each other and different from their decisions for present selves. Experiments involved decisions to drink a disgusting liquid for scientific purposes (Experiment 1) … and defer a lottery prize for a larger one (Experiment 4)."
   - The effects "emerged for real, but not hypothetical, decisions."
   - The full text is not open access, so the direction and amounts of experiment 1 are not claimed. The card shows only "future-you = a stranger", with no amounts.
2. **Peters, J., & Büchel, C. (2010).** Episodic future thinking reduces reward delay discounting through an enhancement of prefrontal-mediotemporal interactions. *Neuron, 66*(1), 138–148. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2010.03.026 (PMID 20399735)
   - With "real subject-specific episodic event cues", choices shifted "toward more future-minded choice behavior"; "a second experiment replicated the behavioral effects". The neural findings are not used.
3. **Hershfield, H. E., Goldstein, D. G., Sharpe, W. F., Fox, J., Yeykelis, L., Carstensen, L. L., & Bailenson, J. N. (2011).** Increasing saving behavior through age-progressed renderings of the future self. *Journal of Marketing Research, 48*(SPL), S23–S37. https://doi.org/10.1509/jmkr.48.SPL.S23 (PMID 24634544)
   - "In four studies … those who interacted with virtual future selves exhibited an increased tendency to accept later monetary rewards over immediate ones."
4. **Rung, J. M., & Madden, G. J. (2018).** Experimental reductions of delay discounting and impulsive choice: A systematic review and meta-analysis. *Journal of Experimental Psychology: General, 147*(9), 1349–1381. https://doi.org/10.1037/xge0000462 (PMID 30148386)
   - 92 articles; "most of the methods reviewed produced significant reductions in discounting, [but] they varied in effect sizes"; episodic future thinking is among them; "most methods were ideal for influencing one-off choices".

How the script uses them (measured / interpretation / illustrative):
- **Measured (Pronin 2008):** "In one experiment about drinking something disgusting, people's choices for future-them looked like choices for a stranger."
- **Interpretation, hedged:** "That distance helps a small reward now beat a bigger one later." This links the self–other distance to discounting (Pronin's experiment 4 was about deferring a prize; Hershfield and Peters measure later-vs-sooner choices). It uses "helps", not "causes".
- **Measured (Peters & Büchel 2010; Hershfield et al. 2011; the Rung & Madden review):** "In studies, picturing specific future events made people's choices more patient." These were lab choices; the review notes most effects were on one-off choices.
- **Advice:** "So picture tomorrow-you clearly, and set one specific step tonight, like shoes by the door." This applies a vivid future plus a specific next step. It promises no outcome. "7:00 run" and the shoes are an illustrative plan.
- **Illustrative:** the cupcake and star, the balance (it tips to "now" as "later" recedes, then to "later" once tomorrow's page is vivid), and the person icons on the card.
