## Research (sources checked 2026-11-18; PubMed abstracts)
1. **Gable, S. L., Reis, H. T., Impett, E. A., & Asher, E. R. (2004).** What do you do when things go right? The intrapersonal and interpersonal benefits of sharing positive events. *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 87*(2), 228–245. https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.87.2.228 (PMID 15301629)
   - "Two studies showed that communicating personal positive events with others was associated with increased daily positive affect and well-being, above and beyond the impact of the positive event itself and other daily events."
   - "when others were perceived to respond actively and constructively (and not passively or destructively) to capitalization attempts, the benefits were further enhanced."
   - "close relationships in which one's partner typically responds to capitalization attempts enthusiastically were associated with higher relationship well-being".
2. **Reis, H. T., Smith, S. M., Carmichael, C. L., Caprariello, P. A., Tsai, F.-F., Rodrigues, A., & Maniaci, M. R. (2010).** Are you happy for me? How sharing positive events with others provides personal and interpersonal benefits. *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 99*(2), 311–329. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0018344 (PMID 20658846)
   - Four experiments and one daily diary study: "sharing good news with others increases the perceived value of those events, especially when others respond enthusiastically, and … enthusiastic responses to shared good news promote the development of trust and a prosocial orientation toward the other."

How the script uses them (associational / measured / advice / illustrative):
- **Associational (Gable 2004, diary studies):** "In diary studies, days when people shared good news had more positive mood, beyond the event itself."
- **Experimental (Reis 2010):** "In experiments, sharing made good news feel more valuable, especially when the listener was enthusiastic."
- **Advice (from both papers; no promised outcome):** "So tonight, tell one person about one small win. And when someone shares theirs, respond with real interest." The second sentence reflects the active-constructive / enthusiastic responses that enhanced benefits.
- **Illustrative:**
  - the orb (good news) and its growth, his friend on her phone, her looking up and raising her hands;
  - the card ("kept it" vs "shared it", direction only, no values);
  - the closed laptop.
