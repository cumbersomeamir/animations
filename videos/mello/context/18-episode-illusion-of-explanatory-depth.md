## Research (sources checked 2026-11-06/07)
1. **Rozenblit, L., & Keil, F. (2002).** The misunderstood limits of folk science: An illusion of explanatory depth. *Cognitive Science, 26*(5), 521–562. https://doi.org/10.1207/s15516709cog2605_1 (abstract read via PubMed, PMID 21442007; author manuscript PMC3062901)
   - "People feel they understand complex phenomena with far greater precision, coherence, and depth than they really do; they are subject to an illusion—an illusion of explanatory depth."
   - "The illusion for explanatory knowledge is most robust where the environment supports real-time explanations with visible mechanisms."
   - The full PDF sits behind publisher/Europe PMC bot checks, which were not bypassed.
2. **Meyers, E. A., Gretton, J. D., Budge, J. R. C., Fugelsang, J. A., & Koehler, D. J. (2023).** Broad effects of shallow understanding: Explaining an unrelated phenomenon exposes the illusion of explanatory depth. *Judgment and Decision Making, 18*, e24. https://doi.org/10.1017/jdm.2023.24 (open access, full text read; three preregistered experiments)
   - "In a standard procedure, people rate their knowledge of some phenomenon (e.g., how a zipper works), then attempt to explain how the phenomenon works …, and then re-rate their knowledge of the phenomenon. The observed effect is that understanding ratings are lower after explaining compared to before."
   - "A person can expose the illusion by attempting to generate a causal explanation for how the phenomenon operates (e.g., how a zipper works). This might be because explanation makes salient the gaps in a person's knowledge."
   - People may overestimate their understanding of a device "because they focus on its visible parts and neglect its hidden parts or because they mistake knowing what an object does for knowing how it works (Rozenblit & Keil, 2002)."
3. **Sundback, G. (1917).** Separable fastener. US Patent 1,219,881 (https://patents.google.com/patent/US1219881A/en, text read). It describes the slider's "Y-shaped channel" and locking members with "an external locking projection … and an internal recess".

How the script uses them:
- "Most of us feel sure, until we explain it step by step" and "In studies, people rated their understanding lower after trying to explain" follow the standard procedure and effect (Meyers et al. 2023, citing Rozenblit & Keil 2002). No numbers are spoken.
- "It's called the illusion of explanatory depth" is the authors' term.
- "So before you say 'I get it', explain it out loud" and "Where you get stuck is what to learn next" restate explanation as the way to expose knowledge gaps (Meyers et al. 2023).
- "With a zipper, it's often the hidden part: the slider's Y-shaped channel" combines the visible/hidden-parts account (Rozenblit & Keil 2002, via Meyers et al. 2023) with the mechanism from the Sundback patent. "Often" keeps it a tendency, not a rule.
- The on-screen rating (6 → 3 on a 1–7 scale) is an illustration of the direction, not a reported mean.
