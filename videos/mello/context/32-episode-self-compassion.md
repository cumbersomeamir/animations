## Research (source checked 2026-11-19; PubMed abstract)
1. **Breines, J. G., & Chen, S. (2012).** Self-compassion increases self-improvement motivation. *Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin, 38*(9), 1133–1143. https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167212445599 (PMID 22645164; abstract read, full text closed access)
   - "Can treating oneself with compassion after making a mistake increase self-improvement motivation? In four experiments…"
   - "Participants in a self-compassion condition, compared to a self-esteem control condition and either no intervention or a positive distraction control condition, … reported greater motivation to make amends and avoid repeating a recent moral transgression (Experiment 2); spent more time studying for a difficult test following an initial failure (Experiment 3)".
   - "taking an accepting approach to personal failure may make people more motivated to improve themselves."
   - The abstract gives no effect sizes, so the card shows direction only (no values).

How the script uses it (measured / gloss / advice / illustrative):
- **Hook / set-up (framing, no claim):** "Too hard on yourself? After an awkward message, the replay can get harsh."
- **Measured (Exp 2):** "In one experiment, people who took a kind, understanding view of something they regretted felt more motivated to make amends." "Something they regretted" glosses "a recent moral transgression"; "kind, understanding view" glosses the self-compassion condition.
- **Measured (Exp 3):** "In another, after failing a test, they studied longer." The abstract: "spent more time studying for a difficult test following an initial failure".
- **Gloss of the paper's conclusion:** "Kindness led to more effort, not less." (more motivation and more study time than the self-esteem and neutral controls; "not less" answers the common worry that self-kindness breeds complacency, which the paper calls "somewhat paradoxically").
- **Advice (no promised outcome):** "So answer the replay kindly, then take one calm step, like a short apology." The apology is an example of making amends (Exp 2's outcome), not a tested intervention.
- **Not claimed:** that self-compassion repairs relationships, improves grades or reduces distress.
- **Illustrative:** the oversized thought cloud, the scribbles and their softening, the message and the apology; the card arrows and bars (direction only — "other groups" vs "kind view"; equal bars before the failure, then the kind-view bar longer).
