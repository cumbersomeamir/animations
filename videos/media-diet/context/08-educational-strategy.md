# 08 · Educational strategy (active direction from 2026-11-02)

Status: **user-approved, active**. This supersedes the campaign-ad direction for educational posts.
Earlier campaign requirements (context/02, context/04: 30 s ad ending "Explore on Google Play") do **not** govern educational pilots.

## Goal
Grow a niche audience with short, genuinely useful lessons. A viewer who never installs Media Diet should still
leave with one correct, memorable explanation. Relevant app posts come occasionally, later and separately.

## Channel niche
Everyday technology mechanisms and explained breakthroughs: how familiar tech actually works, shown as
physical, dimensional mechanisms (cutaways, travelling objects, visual metaphors) rather than talking heads or text slides.

## Format rules (every episode)
- One concept per episode. If it does not fit, narrow the claim; never speed up narration or add false simplifications.
- Structure: question / physical puzzle (0–3 s) → mechanism demonstrated by the character → one concise takeaway.
- Short, comfortably paced narration (~2.3–2.6 words/s overall), concise on-screen captions inside safe areas.
- Simplifications are labelled on screen ("Simplified diagram …") whenever architecture varies in reality.
- Claims are mapped to dated public sources (per-episode SOURCES.md). Unknowns stay unknown.
- App presence: a small, unobtrusive "Media Diet" signature only. No feature tour, no forced store CTA,
  no motivational slogans, no implication that illustrated scenes are Media Diet UI, data or reports.
- Original/licensed audio only; action-timed SFX; music ducked under narration.

## Planned follow-on episodes (titles only — NOT rendered)
1. What does "end-to-end encrypted" actually lock? — Nodo carries a locked box through the same relay; only the two phones hold keys.
2. How does your phone know where you are? — Nodo times signals from satellites on a giant stopwatch dial.
3. Why does Wi-Fi weaken through walls? — Nodo throws signal "rings" through brick, glass and water props.
4. What happens when you tap your card to pay? — inside a card reader's field, a one-time token replaces the card number.
5. How does a scratched QR code still scan? — Nodo patches torn squares using spare error-correction blocks.
6. How do noise-cancelling headphones erase sound? — Nodo flips a wave upside-down on a see-saw to flatten it.
7. How does a touchscreen find your finger? — Nodo walks a glowing capacitive grid that dims where a finger lands.
8. Why do video calls go blurry before they freeze? — Nodo swaps big video crates for smaller ones as a pipe narrows.
9. How do 6-digit login codes change every 30 seconds? — two clocks and a shared secret spin matching code wheels.
10. Why does a phone battery hold less charge over time? — Nodo shuttles ions between shelves that slowly clog.
Each needs its own research, claim-to-source map and accuracy limits before production.
