# Sources — Media Diet english-12 (30 s): "Why is 500 km/h so hard?" — BYD YANGWANG U9 Xtreme, 503.17 km/h

Topic selected 11 Nov 2026 after scanning the previous seven days (4–11 Nov 2026) of official announcements and reliable tech reporting
(feeds: The Verge, Ars Technica, TechCrunch, Engadget, IEEE Spectrum, New Atlas, Electrek, Wired, InsideEVs, NASA, SpaceNews,

DARPA/Caltech ASTRA in-space assembly (strong, but a single truss section and less visible physical stakes), Qantas/Airbus

**Exact topic:** On 1 November 2026 the YANGWANG U9 Xtreme (BYD's luxury brand) reached 503.17 km/h in a single-direction run on a
closed section of the E11 road in Abu Dhabi; the FIA recognised the speed (FIA news, 6 Nov 2026). The episode asks why 500 km/h is
so hard (air drag → power grows with the cube of speed) and how this car got there (four motors, 2.2 MW, temperature-managed runs),
and says plainly that it was a one-way run, not the two-way average used for conventional top-speed records.

**Status:** demonstration/record by a limited-production model (30 units). Not a prototype claim, but not yet a delivered product
either: on 6 Nov 2026 BYD said some customers are at contract-signing and production-planning stage. The record car ran on bespoke
Giti semi-slick tyres; whether customer cars get them is not stated.

## Calculations (derived, not measured) — see the notes in BRIEF-SCRIPT-STORYBOARD.md
- Drag D ∝ V² (NASA drag equation, Cd, ρ, A held constant). Power to push through air P = D · V ∝ V³ (power = force × speed).
- 400 → 500 km/h: (500/400)³ = 1.953 → "nearly double the power". (400 → 503.17: 1.99.)
- 1 % faster: 1.01³ = 1.0303 → "about 3 % more power".
- 503.17 km/h = 139.8 m/s.

## Assumptions (stated, not hidden)
- The cube law describes aerodynamic drag power only, with drag coefficient, frontal area and air density held fixed; it ignores
  rolling resistance, drivetrain losses and the aero changes BYD made. It is a first-principles model, not this car's measured power curve.
- Not claimed: the car's drag coefficient, battery capacity, run duration, delivery dates, price, or any comparison with other cars'
  power. BYD's 30C figure is a manufacturer claim and is not used in narration.
