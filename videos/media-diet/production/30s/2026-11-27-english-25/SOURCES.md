## First principles (explanation)
Momentum is conserved. DART delivered momentum m·v (579 kg × 6.1 km/s). The impact blasted rubble back out of the crater, away from the
asteroid; by recoil (like a rocket's exhaust) that rubble pushed the asteroid further in DART's direction. The measured total push was
β ≈ 3.6 times DART's own momentum, so the rubble's share (β − 1 ≈ 2.6) exceeded DART's share (1) — true across the whole plausible
density range (β ≥ 2.2). That is why the orbit changed far more than a simple "bullet stops in the target" estimate predicted, and why a
kinetic impactor can work better than its mass suggests.
