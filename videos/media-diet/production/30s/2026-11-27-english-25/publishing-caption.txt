We hit an asteroid. Here's the scar. A European probe just found a stadium-sized crater on it. A NASA probe had hit it at six kilometers per second. Its orbit got 33 minutes shorter, far more than predicted. Why? The blast threw rubble backward, like rocket exhaust. That recoil multiplied the push about three and a half times. So the rubble did most of the pushing. Good news for planetary defense.

ESA's Hera images (released 25 Nov 2026) show the 151 m moonlet Dimorphos with one face flattened into a stadium-sized crater and tumbling, after NASA's DART impact on 26 Sep 2022 (579 kg at 6.1 km/s) shortened its orbit by 33 minutes. Measured momentum enhancement β = 3.61 (Cheng et al., Nature 2023; 2.2–4.9 depending on density): the escaping rubble carried more momentum than DART itself. Visuals: illustrative paper reconstruction; Didymos and Dimorphos pose no threat to Earth.
