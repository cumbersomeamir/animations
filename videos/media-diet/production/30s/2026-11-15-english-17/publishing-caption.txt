No joints. Just tension. Colorado State's new robots link up by their claws. So how does a body with no joints hold its shape? Six stiff rods hang in a stretchy web, pushing out while it pulls in. Squash it, and the load spreads, so it bends instead of breaking. Linked up, they loop around pipes and climb ducts. The catch: for now, a human still pilots them. That's tensegrity: strength from pull, not from joints.
