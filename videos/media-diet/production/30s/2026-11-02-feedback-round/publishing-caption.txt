How does a warehouse robot know where it is when GPS struggles indoors?

A lidar-equipped robot sends out laser pulses and times their reflections. Those distances outline the walls and shelves around it. It builds a map, then matches fresh measurements to that map to estimate its position: mapping and locating working together. Move a crate into its way, and new measurements reveal it, so the robot can plan a route around it. (Some robots use other sensors, too.)
