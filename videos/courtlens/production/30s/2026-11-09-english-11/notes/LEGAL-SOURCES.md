# Legal sources: "Can a landlord cut the power to force a tenant out?" (Tamil Nadu, India)

Research date: 2026-11-09 (batch 2026-11-09-english-11). Language of the video: English.

## Topic and jurisdiction choice
- Suggested subject, used as given. Episode history (01–10) has no tenancy, landlord or utility episode.
- **Jurisdiction: Tamil Nadu only.** The narration says "Is that allowed in Tamil Nadu?" and "Other states have their
  own rules". The rule card is headed "TAMIL NADU · LANDLORDS & TENANTS ACT 2017 · S.20".
- Why Tamil Nadu: its current tenancy statute addresses essential services explicitly (s.20). It provides a specific
  local remedy (the Rent Authority, with interim restoration, a one-month inquiry and a penalty) and a separate
  eviction route (the Rent Court, s.21).
- Considered and not used: Maharashtra Rent Control Act 1999, s.29 (similar protection, with a court remedy). India
  Code returned 504 and the Mumbai Port Authority copy returned 503/reset, so no official text could be retrieved.
- Scene: a fictional rented flat in Chennai, Flat 3B, ₹18,000 a month, rent paid. The landlord says "Vacate this week"
  and switches off the flat's supply at the meter. This is general information about one state's Act, not advice on
  a particular tenancy.

## 1. Tamil Nadu Regulation of Rights and Responsibilities of Landlords and Tenants Act, 2017 (TN Act 42 of 2017)
- Source PDF (Gazette pages, with amending Acts 39 of 2018, 22 of 2019, 3 of 2020 and 19 of 2022 appended):
  https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/acts_states/tamil-nadu/2017/2017TN42.pdf
  (sha256 `09a11942f56720463ac19b4c007bc806531601840e632b8e7c8904e86ccc1a91`)
- **s.20, "Cutting off or withholding essential supply or service" (verbatim):**
  > (1) No landlord or tenant either by himself or through any person shall cut-off or withhold any essential supply or
  > service in the premises occupied by the tenant or the landlord.
  > (2) In case of contravention of sub-section (1) and on complaint from the tenant or the landlord, as the case may
  > be, the Rent Authority, after examining the matter, may pass an interim order directing the restoration of supply
  > of essential services immediately pending the inquiry referred to in sub-section (3).
  > (3) The Rent Authority shall conduct an inquiry against the complaint made by the landlord or the tenant, as the
  > case may be, and complete the inquiry within one month of filing of such complaint.
  > (4) The Rent Authority may also levy a penalty on the person responsible for cutting off or withholding the
  > essential supply, which may extend to an amount of loss or damage [incurred but shall not be more than rupees five
  > thousand].
  > (5) The Rent Authority may direct that compensation be paid to the landlord or tenant if it finds that the
  > complaint was made frivolously or vexatiously.
  > Explanation.—For the purpose of this section, essential services includes supply of water, electricity, lights in
  > passages, lifts and on staircases, conservancy, parking, communication links, sanitary services and the like.
- **s.20(4) as amended:** TN Act 39 of 2018, s.14 replaced "as may be prescribed" with "incurred but shall not be more
  than rupees five thousand".
- **s.21(1), eviction:** "A tenant shall not be evicted during the continuance of [tenancy] except in accordance with
  the provisions of sub-section (2)." Sub-section (2): "The Rent Court may, on an application made to it … make an order
  for the recovery of possession of the premises on one or more of the following grounds…" TN Act 19 of 2022 replaced
  "tenancy agreement" with "tenancy" and revised the grounds; it comes into force on notification.
- **s.2(i):** "Rent Authority" means an officer appointed under section 30 (as substituted in 2018).

Narration and screen mapping:
- "The state's tenancy law says no landlord shall cut off essential supplies, electricity included" — s.20(1) and the
  Explanation. Chips: electricity, water, lifts.
- "Eviction needs a Rent Court order, on grounds the Act lists" — s.21(1)–(2). Tag: "Court order only"; split card
  "EVICTION · S.21 · Only by a Rent Court order".
- "So complain to the Rent Authority. It can order the power restored at once, pending its inquiry" — s.20(2). The
  split card adds "penalty ≤ ₹5,000" (s.20(4) as amended) and a sticker "Inquiry: 1 month" (s.20(3)).
- "Attach rent receipts, photos and dates" — practical evidence for the complaint. Not a statutory requirement.

## 2. Checkpoints
- No amendment to TN Act 42 of 2017 after Act 19 of 2022 was found (search 2026-11-09). The commencement notification
  for Act 19 of 2022 was not confirmed; it does not affect s.20, and the s.21 wording change is cosmetic for this
  episode.
- The official Tamil Nadu e-gazette and tenancy portal were unreachable. The PRS copy reproduces the Gazette pages,
  including page headers.
- Scope limits: s.3 excludes certain premises (for example government-owned and religious or charitable premises).
  The fictional flat is an ordinary private rental.

## Claims deliberately not made
- No nationwide rule, no criminal offence, no guaranteed outcome or timeline beyond the Act's own "within one month".
- No statement about other states' laws beyond "other states have their own rules".
