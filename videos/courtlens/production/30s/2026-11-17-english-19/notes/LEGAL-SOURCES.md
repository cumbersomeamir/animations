# Legal sources: "₹18 for their bag?": can a shop charge you for a carry bag? (India)

Research date: 2026-11-17 (episode english-19, batch 19). Language of the video: English.

## Topic decision
- **The proposed topic was already covered.** "Can a shop charge more than MRP in India?" (packaged retail vs
  restaurant service) is episode 02 (2026-11-03, `context/episodes/2026-11-03-mrp-packaged-water.md`). That episode
  covered Packaged Commodities Rules r.18(2) and the Supreme Court's 2017 hotel/restaurant distinction. Batch 17
  already rejected an MRP variant as a repeat.
- **Replacement in the same genre:** one concrete shop scenario with a qualified answer from actual judgments: a carry
  bag added to the bill at the billing counter. Episodes 01–18 have no carry-bag topic.
- **Forum and reach:** consumer commissions, not the Supreme Court.
  - The National Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission (NCDRC) is the apex consumer commission. Its 2024 order sets
    out "law points" as directions to the retailer before it, and sends copies to every State Commission (para 23.8).
  - State and district commissions have since applied it.
- **Supreme Court check (17 Nov 2026):** no Supreme Court ruling on carry-bag charges was found.
  - One search result, a Hindustan Times piece on Magzter, carried a snippet about the Supreme Court setting aside a
    commission judgment. The page returned 403 and no case could be identified, so it is not relied on.
  - No appeal against the NCDRC orders below was found.

## Fact classification
- **NCDRC directions (2024), attributed in the video ("The National Consumer Commission said:"):**
  - there is no obligation to give bags free (23.1)
  - buying one must be optional (23.2)
  - a bag with the seller's name or logo cannot be charged for (23.3)
  - customers may bring empty bags (23.4)
  - notices, including at the entrance, must come before purchase (23.5)
- **Earlier NCDRC order (2020):** the right to know the bag's cost before choosing the shop and the goods. This is
  background; it is not narrated separately.
- **Later state and district orders (2026):** they are consistent with the 2024 directions.
  - **Uttarakhand:** a customer who was told and chose to buy has no claim.
  - **Delhi district:** no duty to give bags free, but notices are required.
  - **Rajasthan:** a plain, unbranded bag can be priced (secondary report only).
  - These are notes only, not narrated.
- **Illustrative:**
  - The amount ₹18 matches the charge in the 2020 Big Bazaar case and the 2026 Uttarakhand case. The 2024 Bata case
    was ₹3.
  - The "SHOP" bag with an "S" roundel, the billing counter, the receipt, the tote and the shopfront are generic; no
    real brand is shown.
- **Practical takeaway:** "Logo bag? Ask for it free. Plain bag? Say no, or bring your own." This follows 23.2–23.4.
- **Not narrated:**
  - Local laws can restrict what bags customers bring (23.4, "unless prohibited by any local laws").
  - Stores may restrict bags that already contain goods from elsewhere but must offer free storage outside (23.7).

## Primary sources
### 1. NCDRC, Bata India Ltd v. Dinesh Parshad Raturi (RP 1715 of 2019) with Bata India Ltd v. Rana Pratap Nath Bhowmik (RP 774 of 2020), decided 27 November 2024
- **Bench:** Dr Inder Jit Singh, Presiding Member.
- **Text:** read in full from Indian Kanoon, https://indiankanoon.org/doc/153715230/ (fetched page sha256
  `cee94d7e7354efcda715bbade983a6ca7a5d34985ea0e14aeef5322e9fc35e12`). The same order is quoted in the Delhi district
  order (source 4).
- **Para 23:** "both the RPs are partly allowed with the following directions".
- **23.1:** "... in the absence of any law or rules or regulations in force, sellers like the Petitioner ... is/are under
  no obligation to provide a free carry bag(s) ... However, this is subject to observance of following conditions."
- **23.2:** "The purchase of such carry bag(s) ... has to be made optional ... the customer has the choice, whether to buy
  such carry bag or not."
- **23.3:** the bag sold "cannot have its name or logo or any kind of advertisement or slogan ... otherwise, the
  Petitioner cannot charge for such carry bag(s) and same shall be supplied free of cost." It continues: "...
  cannot charge for the carry bag bearing its/anybody else's name or logo ... even if it claims that it is only
  partially recovering the cost".
- **23.4:** "The Petitioner cannot/shall not put any restrictions on customers bringing empty carry bags ... (unless
  prohibited by any local laws) inside the store(s)".
- **23.5:** "multiple notices at prominent places ... including the one at the entrance gate(s) itself and the one at
  the payment counter(s) ... putting such notices on the invoice is not sufficient ... the customer must have the
  notice before the purchase of the goods and/or the carry bag".
- **23.8:** copies to "all other State Commissions ... for information".
- **23.10:** sums already paid were not recovered; the ₹5,000 legal-aid deposit was set aside.

### 2. NCDRC, Big Bazaar (Future Retail Ltd.) v. Ashok Kumar, RP 975 of 2020 (lead; with RP 976–988 of 2020), decided 22 December 2020
- **Bench:** Dinesh Singh, Presiding Member.
- **Text:** read in full from Indian Kanoon, https://indiankanoon.org/doc/161722075/ (fetched page sha256
  `c097a0f08589a3ae85e28a5c8e73ca3f001b5b676e75c21571726252013ca5d0`).
- **Para 6:** "charging additional cost (Rs. 18/- in this case) for carry bag(s) ... has been held by the two Fora below
  ... to be deficiency and unfair trade practice".
- **Para 14:** "the consumer has the right to know that there will be an additional cost for carry bags ... and also to
  know the salient specifications and price of the carry bags, before he exercises his choice of patronizing a
  particular retail outlet and before he makes his selection of goods".
- **Para 15:** the practice must be discontinued under s.39(1)(g), Consumer Protection Act 2019. "The notice or
  information cannot be at the occasion of making payment".
- **Para 21:** the critique applies to "similarly / better placed traders".

### 3. Uttarakhand State Commission, Dr Ramesh Singh Danu v. Vishal Mega Mart, SC/5/A/25/2024, pronounced 30 October 2026
- **Bench:** Kumkum Rani, President; B.S. Manral, Member.
- **Text:** order PDF published by Bar & Bench,
  https://images.assettype.com/barandbench/2026-11-03/v0zydooh/Ramesh_Singh_Danu_Vs_Vishal_Mega_Mart.pdf (sha256
  `246f31ad1167318507c8119ce9aaca955685f4d9094073f4bd020e044f16bf45`).
- **Facts:** ₹18 was charged for a bag at billing.
- **Para 6:** no averment that the customer was forced; he chose to buy the bag "at his own free will".
- **Para 7:** NCDRC 2020 was distinguished. The store's defence was that bag prices were displayed at the billing
  counter and in the store, and the customer did not claim he was unaware of the charge.
  "the shopkeeper is under no obligation to supply carry bags and that too, without any cost and there is no law which
  mandates providing free carry bag to the customer."
- **Outcome:** the appeal was dismissed. The order does not discuss NCDRC 2024 or logos.

### 4. District Commission, South Delhi, Priti Agarwal v. Bata India Pvt Ltd, CC 151 of 2023, order dated 26 May 2026
- **Text:** order PDF via LiveLaw,
  https://www.livelaw.in/pdf_upload/2026/07/18/priti-agarwal-v-bata-india-private-limited-687692.pdf (sha256
  `86f731ce4f774d7641da7650044873881a99e0f6cdf699e3f625de1da2b7f579`).
- **Para 25:** stores are not "duty bound or under obligation to provide a carry bag free of cost".
- **Para 29:** quotes the NCDRC 2024 directions 23.1–23.6.
- **Para 30:** Bata was found deficient for not ensuring that notices per the NCDRC directions are displayed in every
  store.

## Secondary (orientation only)
- **LiveLaw, Rajasthan State Commission, D-Mart v. Kapil Dev, FA/385/2025 (reported Aug 2026):** a plain bag without a
  logo may be charged a reasonable price. The full order was not read.
- **Other reports:** LiveLaw and SCC Online reports of the 2020 NCDRC order; Bar & Bench, Lawchakra and Lawstreet on
  the Uttarakhand order; Business Today on the Delhi Bata order.
