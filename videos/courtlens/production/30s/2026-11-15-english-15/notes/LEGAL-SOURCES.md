# Legal sources: "Can your employer keep your original certificates?" (India)

Research date: 2026-11-15 (episode english-15). Language of the video: English.

## Topic decision
- **Proposed topic, used as given.** Episode history (01–14) has no episode on employment documents or certificate
  retention. The nearest is 09 (a hotel keeping your Aadhaar), which is about ID at a hotel and rests on different
  rules.
- **Scope (as the brief asked: one clear case, accurate jurisdiction):** the video rests on one judgment,
  *A. John Paul v. State* (Madras High Court, 2012). It is named on screen ("MADRAS HIGH COURT · 2012 · teachers v.
  their school").
  - It is a single-judge order binding in Tamil Nadu and persuasive elsewhere.
  - The video does not claim a nationwide statute or a blanket rule.
- **Custody, consent and coercion, as the video frames it:**
  - **custody with consent:** handing originals over at joining. The court treated this as *entrustment*.
  - **coercion:** keeping them after you leave to force a payment ("like a ransom"), which the court held unlawful.
  - **the lawful route for money owed:** the employer can sue (recovery proceedings).

## Fact classification
- **Judicial facts (quoted below):** the Madras High Court's holdings and its direction to the police. The Delhi High
  Court's 2018 judgment is supporting context in the notes and is not narrated.
- **Illustrative assumptions:**
  - the office drawer, the "HR" tray and the "PAY UP FIRST" tag
  - the resignation letter
  - the degree certificate, which is generic: no real institution, name or seal
  - the police shield, which is generic: no real insignia
- **Practical suggestions, not legal duties:** "get a signed receipt, ask for them back in writing, and keep every
  reply". These build the evidence that mattered in the case: proof of entrustment and a demand for return that was
  refused. The teachers had asked for return, then complained to police, then went to the High Court when police did
  not act.
- **Not claimed:**
  - that retention during employment is always illegal (the court's reasoning is about retention after the employee
    leaves and asks)
  - that every refusal is a crime (the order speaks of a prima facie offence for police investigation)
  - that a contract clause can or cannot ever permit retention anywhere in India. Madras treated the clause as void
    (TP Act s.6); the Delhi HC 2018 judgment relied on the bond having no retention clause.

## 1. A. John Paul & others v. State rep. by Inspector of Police, E-5 Foreshore Estate Police Station, Chennai & another
- Madras High Court, Crl.OP No. 9920 of 2012, decided 10.07.2012, S. Nagamuthu J.
- **Source:** Indian Kanoon reproduction, https://indiankanoon.org/doc/195026295/ (fetched 2026-11-15; page capture
  sha256 `9e3dcd2520f066721b5f041b289f6c5384a207548406a9a155db5ca6405229a0`, kept in the session scratchpad, not
  committed).
- **Facts (para 2–3):**
  - Teachers at a private CBSE school submitted original SSLC, +2 and degree certificates at joining, as the
    management insisted.
  - After resigning (2010/2011) they asked for them back; the management refused.
  - The management relied on an agreement requiring three months' salary to be repaid for a mid-year exit.
- **Holdings (verbatim):**
  - para 10: "the certificates of the petitioners cannot be retained like a ransom. The retention of the certificates
    by the second respondent Management, in my considered opinion, is unlawful which makes out an offence under
    Section 406 I.P.C."
  - para 17: "the certificates were only entrusted. If it is a mere entrustment and not a transfer, then the second
    respondent Management has got no authority in law to retain the same after the Teachers have resigned their job
    and the trust is withdrawn."
  - para 23: "it is always open for the second respondent Management to initiate proceedings against the petitioners
    for recovery of the amount in terms of the agreement. But, for the failure of the petitioners to pay the said
    amount, their certificates cannot be retained. ... No other person including the employer has got right to retain
    the certificates after the candidate has left his service. As and when he leaves, the certificates should be
    returned to him"
  - para 24: "this Criminal Original Petition is allowed with a direction to the first respondent police to register a
    case under Section 406 I.P.C., against the second respondent on the complaints of the petitioners dated
    28.03.2012 and investigate the matter and then file a final report in accordance with law."
- **Current law note:** IPC ss.405–406 (criminal breach of trust) were replaced from 1 July 2024 by the Bharatiya
  Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 (s.316 per standard concordances). The bare BNS text was not fetched in this round. The video
  says "breach of trust" and cites no section number.

## 2. Supporting (notes only): Dr. Devender Pal Singh Tomar & Anr. v. Union of India & Ors.
- Delhi High Court, W.P.(C) 7230/2017, decided 16.03.2018, Rekha Palli J.
- **Source:** https://indiankanoon.org/doc/187412563/ (capture sha256
  `0cc465592696bc0fab334cc17f75ffd53e350dd75311fe0c08701a43ac53c728`).
- **Facts:** PG medical trainees deposited original MBBS certificates and signed a ₹25 lakh service bond.
- **Para 9:** "there is no condition in the said bond entitling the Respondent Nos. 1 and 2 to retain the Petitioners'
  original documents and, therefore, ... the Respondent Nos. 1 and 2 cannot, by way of ransom, retain the said
  documents. Even though the Respondent Nos. 1 and 2 may be free to initiate steps to recover the said amount in
  accordance with law, the action ... to retain the said documents in the absence of any condition in the bond, is
  wholly arbitrary, illegal and liable to be set aside."
- **Para 10** notes similar views by the Kerala HC (*Tissna Paul*, WP(C) 24526/2013) and the Madras HC
  (*S. Rajesh*, WP(C) 12716-18/2008). Those were not read.
- **Not used in narration:** a bond/education context (government as bond-holder). Its reasoning depends partly on
  the bond lacking a retention clause.
