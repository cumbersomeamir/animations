# Legal sources: "Can a restaurant force you to pay a service charge?" (India)

Research date: 2026-11-05 (batch 2026-11-06-english-06). Language of the video: English.
Scope: a service charge added to a restaurant bill, compared with a voluntary tip and with government tax (GST).
The bill in the video is fictional: dinner ₹1,000 + service charge 10% ₹100 + GST 5% ₹55 = ₹1,155. Without the
service charge it would be ₹1,000 + GST 5% ₹50 = ₹1,050. This is general information, not advice on any one case.

Status key:
- **VERIFIED-PRIMARY:** the document itself, from an official host
- **VERIFIED-OFFICIAL:** an official government release
- **CHECKPOINT:** could not be confirmed from a primary source and is stated as unconfirmed

## 1. CCPA service-charge guidelines, 4 July 2022 [VERIFIED-PRIMARY]
- Central Consumer Protection Authority, F.No. J-25/57/2022-CCPA, Krishi Bhawan, New Delhi, dated **4th July, 2022**:
  "Guidelines to prevent unfair trade practices and protection of consumer interest with regard to levy of service
  charge in hotels and restaurants" (Consumer Protection Act, 2019).
- Official PDF: https://ccpa.doca.gov.in/files/guidelines-to-prevent.pdf
  (sha256 `0ed42f76ee9aa4a3a291d03514d00357b90f83791d4b7e2b3efde22e3df3f022`)
- **Para 7 (verbatim):**
  > (i) No hotel or restaurant shall add service charge automatically or by default in the bill.
  > (ii) Service charge shall not be collected from consumers by any other name.
  > (iii) No hotel or restaurant shall force a consumer to pay service charge and shall clearly inform the consumer
  > that service charge is voluntary, optional and at consumer's discretion.
  > (iv) No restriction on entry or provision of services based on collection of service charge shall be imposed on
  > consumers.
  > (v) Service charge shall not be collected by adding it along with the food bill and levying GST on the total
  > amount.
- **Para 9, consumer remedies (summarised; verbatim wording in the PDF):**
  1. ask the restaurant to remove the service charge from the bill
  2. complain to the National Consumer Helpline (NCH) on **1915** or through the NCH app
  3. file with the Consumer Commission, including through e-Daakhil
  4. complain to the District Collector, or email the CCPA
- The guidelines also say that the menu price is taken to include the food and applicable taxes. A separate charge
  on top of that is an unjustified cost.
- Narration uses: "can't be added by default" (7(i)), "made compulsory" (7(iii)), "charged under another name"
  (7(ii)), and "Ask them to remove it … call 1915" (9(i)–(ii)).

## 2. Delhi High Court, 28 March 2025: guidelines upheld [VERIFIED-PRIMARY]
- *National Restaurant Association of India v. Union of India* and *Federation of Hotels & Restaurant Associations of
  India v. Union of India*, W.P.(C) 10683/2022 and W.P.(C) 10867/2022. Single Judge. Date of decision: **28th March,
  2025**. 131 pages.
- Copy hosted by the Department of Consumer Affairs:
  https://consumeraffairs.gov.in/public/upload/admin/cmsfiles/whatsnews/Delhi_High_Court_order_on_mandatory_collection_of_service_charge_by_restaurants_whatsnews.pdf
  (sha256 `ac075589e6b91492f0f61836118f5f1d0cb8925b6d5b024c44be166af8bc8909`)
- **Para 174 conclusions (verbatim extracts):**
  - "(iii) Service charge or TIP as is colloquially referred, is a voluntary payment by the customer. It cannot be
    compulsory or mandatory."
  - "(iv) The collection of service charge and use of different terminologies for the said charge is misleading and
    deceptive in nature. The same constitutes an unfair trade practice under Section 2(47) of the CPA, 2019"
  - "(viii) … if consumers wish to pay any voluntary Tip for services which they had enjoyed, the same would
    obviously not be barred. The amount however, ought not to be added by default in the bill/invoice and should
    be left to the customer's discretion."
  - "(ix) … Terminology such as 'voluntary contribution', 'staff contribution', 'staff welfare fund' or similar
    terminology can be permitted. The use of the word 'service charge' is misleading as consumers tend to confuse
    the same with service tax or GST or some other tax which is imposed and collected by the government."
- "175. The guidelines framed by the CCPA are thus valid and are in the interest of the consumers and the same are
  upheld." "177. The writ petitions … are dismissed with costs of Rs.1 lakh each to be deposited with Central
  Consumer Protection Authority …"
- Narration uses: "upheld by the Delhi High Court in 2025", "Like a tip, a service charge is your choice". On screen:
  the "Upheld · Delhi HC, 2025" stamp.

## 3. What applies today: enforcement after the judgment [VERIFIED-OFFICIAL]
- **PIB, 10 Jan 2026** (Ministry of Consumer Affairs), "Mandatory Levy of Service Charge by Restaurants Violates
  Consumer Law: CCPA". CCPA took suo motu cognizance against **27 restaurants**, imposed penalties of up to ₹50,000,
  and directed refunds and billing changes. PDF copy on the DoCA site (checked):
  https://consumeraffairs.gov.in/public/upload/admin/cmsfiles/pressRelease/Mandatory_Levy_of_Service_Charge_by_Restaurants_Violates_Consumer_Law_CCPApress_release.pdf
- **PIB, 25 Mar 2026**: a CCPA advisory says "LPG charges", "gas surcharge", "fuel cost recovery" and similar default
  charges are in the nature of service charge or additional fees. Levying them by default violates the 04.07.2022
  guidelines. https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2245074
- **PIB, 19 Jul 2026**: CCPA suo motu action against **41 restaurants** for levying service charge by default, based
  on NCH complaints supported by bills. It restates that the Delhi High Court, "in its judgment dated 28 March 2025 …
  upheld the validity of the CCPA's Guidelines on Service Charge and held that mandatory collection of service charge
  is contrary to law". https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2286326
- **PIB, 4 Jul 2022** (issue of the guidelines; NCH 1915 and e-Daakhil remedies):
  https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleseDetailm.aspx?PRID=1839133

## 4. Litigation status: the appeal [CHECKPOINT]
- NRAI and FHRAI appealed the single-judge judgment to a Division Bench of the Delhi High Court (filed about April
  2025). News reports (LiveLaw, Bar & Bench, Courtbook) describe hearings in May and August 2025.
- As of 2026-11-05 I found **no Division Bench ruling, stay or set-aside order**. The official 19 Jul 2026 PIB release
  still relies on the 28 Mar 2025 judgment and reports fresh enforcement. The video therefore says the guidelines were
  "upheld … in 2025" and treats them as applying today. It does not claim the appeal is finished.
- Unresolved: the Division Bench appeal status could not be confirmed on the court's own site (delhihighcourt.nic.in
  hosts are unreachable from this environment). Re-check before reuse.

## 5. GST on restaurant food [VERIFIED-OFFICIAL]
- GST Council, "GST on Hotel & Restaurant Services since 1980" (2024): "Standalone restaurants, including takeaway:
  5% without ITC" and "Restaurant services are generally taxable @5% without ITC".
  https://gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/2024-04/restaurantservices_vns-i.pdf
  (sha256 `d9d81f7dcb1670bc012bd693f05087fff11a19e8c69031b6bd53a8b1648513be`)
- 56th GST Council FAQs (PIB, 3 Sep 2025; rate changes from 22 Sep 2025) do not change standalone-restaurant GST;
  the restaurant-related item is hotel accommodation up to ₹7,500, which moves to 5% without ITC.
  https://gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-09/faq_0.pdf
  (sha256 `99cd227a6de7606c9680ef87830abc936d5d7dd78c5c51b9cef72a2a75fa0bb8`)
- Limit: restaurants in hotels with a room tariff of ₹7,500 or more have a different rate. The video's bill is a
  standalone restaurant, so it shows GST 5%. The narration only says "G S T is a government tax, which you do pay"
  and gives no rate.

## 6. National Consumer Helpline [VERIFIED-OFFICIAL]
- NCH toll-free 1915 (also 1800-11-4000); contact page re-checked for the previous episode on 2026-11-05:
