# Legal sources: "Can a bank refuse to exchange your torn note?" (India)

Research date: 2026-11-13 (episode english-13). Language of the video: English.

## Topic decision
- **Proposed topic:** "Does NO RETURNS mean a shop can refuse a defective product?" The next-episode brief asked for
  defective vs change-of-mind goods, a broken product, receipt, conflicting sign, evidence and a complaint route.
- **Already covered:**
  - Episode 01 (2026-11-02, English) covered an online seller's "NO REFUNDS" on a broken item. It used E-Commerce
    Rules 6(3)/7(4), the defective vs change-of-mind split, and the invoice + photo + written request.
  - Episode 04 (2026-11-04, Hindi) covered a shop bill's "no refund" on a defective kettle. It used CPA 2019
    s.2(47)(viii) (the bill's period or 30 days) and NCH 1915.
  - Repeating either would duplicate the subject, so a genuinely new topic in the same genre was chosen.
- **Chosen topic (same story criteria):**
  - a damaged item (a torn ₹500 note)
  - a conflicting counter sign ("NO ACCOUNT? NO EXCHANGE")
  - the rule that decides it
  - a distinction that stops the video promising an automatic full refund: soiled (two pieces, nothing missing) vs
    mutilated (a piece missing → full / half / nothing)
  - a practical complaint route (written complaint → RBI Ombudsman)
- **History check:** no earlier Courtlens episode covers currency exchange. Episode 07 (failed ATM) is banking but a
  different rule and remedy.
- **Jurisdiction:** all of India. These are Reserve Bank of India directions to banks, not a state rule.

## Fact classification
- **Regulatory facts:** quoted below (RBI Master Direction, Note Refund Rules, RB-IOS 2026 FAQ).
- **Illustrative assumptions:**
  - The bank's sign, the teller and the single ₹500 note are a fictional scene.
  - The three "what's left" sizes in the full/half/nothing beat (about 90%, 60% and 27% of the note) are examples
    chosen to fall in each band. They are not measurements.
  - The note is a stylised prop (no portrait or security features), not a reproduction of a real banknote.
- **Simplifications in the narration (accurate for the depicted case, qualified here):**
  - "Exchanged free at the counter" is the small-number rule: up to 20 soiled notes and ₹5,000 a day (para 6.1.1). Bulk
    tenders go against a receipt and may carry reasonable service charges (6.1.2).
  - "Full, half, or nothing" applies to notes of ₹50 and above (NRR rule 8(2)). Below ₹50 the outcome is full value
    or rejection (rule 8(1)). The prop is a ₹500 note.
  - "It's still accepted" is the general rule (para 4.1). Small Finance Banks (first two years) and Payment Banks may
    exchange mutilated/imperfect notes at their option (para 1.3).
  - Notes that are extremely brittle, charred or stuck together are not accepted at branches; they go to an RBI Issue
    Office (para 5). Deliberately cut or tampered notes are rejected (para 10.3).
- **Practical suggestion:** "Make a written complaint" follows from para 9 and from RB-IOS 2026, which requires the
  complainant to first approach the bank with proof of the complaint.

## 1. RBI Master Direction – Facility for Exchange of Notes and Coins
- RBI/DCM/2026-27/395, DCM (NE) No.G-2/08.07.18/2026-27, April 01, 2026, updated as on October 16, 2026 (and
  September 01, 2026).
- https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=13362 (fetched 2026-11-13; capture sha256
  `04d84314f2c1d981d55fc3123554228766e9423e47d71f952286788d71533e62`, kept in the session scratchpad, not committed)
- Verbatim:
  - **1.1** "All bank branches in all parts of the country are mandated to provide following customer services ...
    1.1.2 Exchanging soiled/ mutilated/ imperfect notes"
  - **1.3** "Banks shall ensure that all their branches provide facility for exchange of notes and coins not only to
    their customers but also others. Small Finance Banks (up to two years from the commencement of their banking
    business) and Payment Banks may exchange mutilated and imperfect notes at their option."
  - **1.7** "All branches shall provide the above facilities to members of public without any discrimination on all
    working days."
  - **3** "A 'soiled note' means a note which has become dirty due to normal wear and tear and also includes a
    two-piece note pasted together wherein both the pieces presented belong to the same note and form the entire note
    with no essential feature missing. These notes shall be accepted over bank counters for exchange or credit to
    accounts ..."
  - **4.1** "A mutilated note is a note of which a portion is missing or which is composed of more than two pieces.
    Mutilated notes may be presented at any of the bank branches. The notes so presented shall be accepted, exchanged
    and adjudicated in accordance with NRR, 2009 (as amended in 2018)."
  - **5** Extremely brittle, charred or inseparably stuck notes "shall not be accepted by the bank branches for
    exchange", and holders are directed to the RBI Issue Office.
  - **6.1.1** "Where the number of notes presented by a person is up to 20 pieces with a maximum value of ₹5,000 per
    day, banks shall exchange them over the counter, free of charge."
  - **6.2.2** "Where the number of notes presented by a person is up to 10 pieces, bank branches shall normally
    adjudicate the notes as per the procedure laid down in Part III of NRR, 2009 (as amended in 2018) and pay the
    exchange value over the counter."
  - **9** "In case of grievance of a tenderer against a specific bank, a complaint may be lodged with the bank branch
    concerned. If the bank does not respond within a period of 30 days after lodging of the complaint or rejects the
    complaint wholly/ partly or if the complainant is not satisfied with the response/ resolution given by the bank
    branch, complaint can be lodged with the Ombudsman, under 'The Reserve Bank - Integrated Ombudsman Scheme, 2021'.
    Complaints can be filed online on https://cms.rbi.org.in ..."
    - The direction still names the 2021 scheme. Per the RBI FAQ below, RB-IOS 2026 replaced it from 1 July 2026.
  - **13** Branches must display "a board indicating the availability of note and coin exchange facility with the
    legend, 'SOILED/ MUTILATED NOTES AND COINS ARE ACCEPTED AND EXCHANGED HERE'". On screen this is shortened to
    "NOTES EXCHANGED HERE".
  - **14.2** "Non-compliance with any provision of this Master Direction shall be viewed as violation of instructions
    ... and penalty, as applicable ... shall be imposed." This is not used in the video.

## 2. Reserve Bank of India (Note Refund) Rules, 2009, as amended by the Note Refund (Amendment) Rules, 2018
- RBI consolidated text dated 28 Dec 2018: https://rbi.org.in/scripts/PublicationsView.aspx?id=18770 (read via a
  fetch summary; quotes are partial).
- **Rule 8(2), notes of ₹50 and above:**
  - full value if the single largest undivided piece is more than 80% of the note's area
  - **half value** from 40% up to 80%
  - "no value shall be payable, and the claim shall be rejected" below 40%
  - Table row for ₹500 (150 x 66 mm, 99 cm²): full value from 80 cm², half value from 40 cm².
- **Rule 8(1), notes below ₹50:** full value if the largest piece exceeds 50%; otherwise rejected. There is no half
  value.
- **Rule 6(3):** rejects claims where the note was deliberately cut, torn, defaced or altered, or where fraudulent
  intention appears.
- Master Direction para 2.1 (RBI Act ss.28/58(2)): "no person is entitled as a right to recover ... the value of any
  lost, stolen, mutilated or imperfect currency note". The refund is "a matter of grace" under the Rules. This is why
  the video says "paid by what's left" and never promises a full refund.

## 3. Reserve Bank – Integrated Ombudsman Scheme, 2026 (RB-IOS 2026)
- RBI FAQ: https://www.rbi.org.in/commonman/Upload/English/FAQs/PDFs/RBIOS01072026.pdf (fetched 2026-11-13; sha256
  `db923b0fe96466e98510a151404c3022b5d8e3a48b51e304c142e354ba0dca66`)
  - Q1: "It comes into force with effect from July 1, 2026. RB-IOS, 2026 replaces the Reserve Bank - Integrated
    Ombudsman Scheme, 2021."
  - Q16 (non-maintainable): "Complaints where the complainant has not first approached the concerned Regulated Entity
    with proof of such complaint"; and "Complaints filed before the expiry of 30 days or the time specified by RBI,
    NPCI or Card Network guidelines, if applicable, whichever is higher, unless the complainant has received a
    reply/resolution and is dissatisfied."
  - Q17: "A complaint can be filed before the RBI Ombudsman if the complainant has not received any reply within 30
    days ... or if the complainant is not satisfied with the reply/resolution ... The complaint must be made to the RBI
    Ombudsman within 90 days from the date on which the above timeline expires or the date of the last communication
    from the concerned Regulated Entity, whichever is later."
  - Q7/Q10: online complaints at https://cms.rbi.org.in
- The full scheme PDF (https://rbidocs.rbi.org.in/rdocs/content/pdfs/SCHEME16012026_A.pdf, linked from FAQ Q35)
  returned an empty reply or a human-verification page from this environment. It was not read, and the scheme's
  clause text is not quoted here.
