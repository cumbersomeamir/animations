## Source (official)
**Supreme Court of India, *Taj Mahal Hotel v. United India Insurance Company Ltd. & Ors.*, Civil Appeal No. 8611 of
2019** (arising out of SLP (C) 11213 of 2018), judgment dated **14 November 2019**. Bench: Mohan M. Shantanagoudar J.
(author) and Ajay Rastogi J. Reportable.
- Official PDF: https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2018/15431/15431_2018_14_1502_18237_Judgement_14-Nov-2019.pdf
  (55 pages, digitally signed 2019.11.14; downloaded 2026-11-19, sha256
  `44b4453a840c252ede4c86c91c073d0dba1779cc70adb4a642fcf19a7db26104`).
- Text extracted with `pdftotext -layout` and read for paras 3, 15–19, 21–31.
- **Facts (para 3):** on 1 Aug 1998 a guest's insured Maruti Zen was handed to the valet at the appellant hotel in New
  Delhi. The parking tag said parking was at the owner's risk. The car was stolen. The appeal was dismissed and the
  hotel held liable.

## Holdings used (verbatim excerpts)
- **Para 18.3, valet custody creates a bailment:**
  > in a situation where the hotel actively undertakes to park the vehicle for the owner, keep it in safe custody and
  > return it upon presentation of a parking slip in a manner such that the parking of the vehicle is beyond the
  > control of the owner, a contract of bailment exists.
- **Para 18.2, self-parking is different:** where a facility "had only licensed out its premises for parking, and
  left it to the discretion of the vehicle owner as to where to park the car … it cannot be considered that possession
  has been handed over or that a relationship of bailment has been created." Para 18 calls this "only a
  licensor-licensee relationship where laws of bailment or the prima facie liability rule cannot be applied."
- **Paras 18.7–18.8, even if free:** "a contract of bailment under Section 148 may be gratuitous … valet parking
  service, even if offered gratuitously, benefits the hotel … the Appellant-hotel cannot refute the existence of
  bailment by contending that its valet parking service was purely complimentary".
- **Para 19, the burden of proof:** "the failure of the Appellant to deliver the car … raises a prima facie case of
  negligence against it. Thus, the burden of proof is on the hotel (bailee) to show that efforts were undertaken by it
  to take reasonable care of the vehicle bailed".
- **Para 28, no contracting out of negligence:** "the standard of care required to be taken by the hotel as a bailee
  under Section 151 is sacrosanct and cannot be contracted out of … Where the hotel or its servants have actively
  connived against or acted negligently in safeguarding the vehicles delivered for valet parking, 'owner's risk'
  clauses in the parking token will not come to their rescue."
- **Para 28, limits:**
  - hotels may print disclaimers for loss "occasioned by acts of a third party, contributory negligence or by force
    majeure events", but only "subject to the hotel discharging its initial burden of proving that it fulfilled the
    standard of care"
  - not liable for acts of God, legal seizure or the guest's own negligence
- **Para 30(i)–(ii), summary:** "the hotel-owner cannot contract out of liability for its negligence or that of its
  servants in respect of a vehicle of its guest in any circumstance … Even where there is a general or specific
  exemption clause, there remains a prima facie burden of proof on the hotel".
- **Scope (para 28):** "the above observations are limited to … the liability of hotels as bailees for vehicles
  handed over to them for valet parking."

## Scope notes (kept out of the narration)
1. **Hotels:** the ruling is expressly limited to hotels as bailees for valet parking (para 28). The narration says
   "a hotel's valet". Consumer commissions have applied the reasoning elsewhere: North Chennai District Commission,
   Dec 2023 (hotel); Bangalore District Commission, Jul 2024 (restaurant valet damage), per LiveLaw reports. Those
   orders were not read in full and are not narrated.
2. **Not absolute liability:** strict liability was rejected (para 31). The rule is prima facie negligence. A hotel
   that proves due care can rely on disclaimers for third-party acts or force majeure (para 28). The video says "must
   prove it took proper care" and "can't excuse its own negligence". It never says the hotel always pays.
3. **Later law:** searches on 2026-11-19 found no later Supreme Court decision changing this. Later commission orders
   follow it.
