# Legal sources: "Can a shop charge above the printed MRP for a packaged water bottle?" (INDIA)

Research date: 2026-11-03. Jurisdiction: India. Scope: packaged goods bought at a retail shop. The hotel/restaurant
case is only distinguished.
Status key: **VERIFIED-PRIMARY** = read in the Gazette or judgment text from an official host;
**VERIFIED-OFFICIAL** = official government page; **SECONDARY** = reputable reproduction or report; **CHECKPOINT** = open item.

## 1. Retail rule: Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules, 2011 [VERIFIED-PRIMARY]
Made under the Legal Metrology Act, 2009, s.52. Principal rules: **G.S.R. 202(E), 7 March 2011**.
All texts below were downloaded from the Department of Consumer Affairs "Acts & Rules" page
(https://consumeraffairs.gov.in/pages/legal-metrology-act).

**Rule 18(2), verbatim** (principal Gazette scan, page 57; https://consumeraffairs.gov.in/public/upload/files/8_1732871406.pdf,
sha256 `48cd6acd…f890`):
> (2) No retail dealer or other person including manufacturer, packer, importer and wholesale dealer shall make any
> sale of any commodity in packed form at a price exceeding the retail sale price thereof.

Rule 18(5) (same page) adds: "No wholesale dealer or retail dealer or other person shall obliterate, smudge or alter
the retail sale price, indicated by the manufacturer or the packer or the importer…"

**Rule 2(m), retail sale price (MRP)** as substituted by the 2017 amendment, **G.S.R. 629(E), 23 June 2017**
(https://consumeraffairs.gov.in/public/upload/files/8(xii)_0_1732871346.pdf, sha256 `eabd56ec…7d28`):
> (m) "retail sale price" means the maximum price at which the commodity in packaged form may be sold to the
> consumer inclusive of all taxes;

**Rule 6(1)(e), the MRP must be printed:** the 2017 amendment required the declaration to "clearly indicate that it
is the maximum retail price inclusive of all taxes". The 2021 amendment, **G.S.R. 779(E), 3 Nov 2021, in force
1 Apr 2022** (https://consumeraffairs.gov.in/public/upload/files/230946_1732871433.pdf, sha256 `2c443834…17d9`),
replaced the rupees-and-paise wording and illustrations with "in Indian currency". The "maximum retail price
inclusive of all taxes" requirement remains. The same 2017 amendment inserted Rule 18(2A), which bans different
MRPs on an identical pre-packaged commodity.

**Currency check, every amendment through today:** DoCA lists the 2025–2026 amendments as the Amendment Rules of
24 Oct 2025 (medical devices, Rules 7/33), G.S.R. 881(E) 2 Dec 2025 (pan masala, Rule 26),
G.S.R. 128(E) 13 Feb 2026 and G.S.R. 312(E) 27 Apr 2026 (e-commerce country-of-origin filter, Rule 6(10A)),
G.S.R. 418(E) 29 May 2026 (registration, Rule 27) and **G.S.R. 826(E) 21 Sep 2026** (veg/non-veg dot, Rule 6(4A);
latest). I read each operative text. **None amends Rule 2(m), Rule 6(1)(e) or Rule 18(2).**
I also checked the 2022–2023 notifications G.S.R. 226(E), 859(E), 640(E) and 722(E), the last of which adds
Rule 26(g) on loose e-commerce goods. None changes Rule 2(m), 6(1)(e) or 18(2).

## 2. Restaurants and hotels: a different case [VERIFIED-PRIMARY]
Supreme Court of India, *Federation of Hotel & Restaurant Associations of India v. Union of India*, Civil Appeal
No. 21790 of 2017 (with CA 21791/2017), judgment of **12 December 2017** (R.F. Nariman and Navin Sinha, JJ.).
PDF: https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2015/27941/27941_2015_Judgement_12-Dec-2017.pdf (sha256 `50caa2a3…eba`).
Para 17:
> We are, therefore, of the view that neither the Standards of Weights and Measures Act, 1976 read with the
> enactment of 1985, or the Legal Metrology Act, 2009, would apply so as to interdict the sale of mineral water in
> hotels and restaurants at prices which are above the MRP.

The reasoning quotes the single judge: a customer "does not enter a hotel or a restaurant to make a simple purchase
of these commodities". The supply is part of a service.

The video says only "Restaurants and hotels serving you are a different case." It does not claim that restaurants
may charge anything. Some later consumer-commission orders have penalised restaurants under consumer law, so the
exact outcome there is outside this episode. Examples (SECONDARY): a Chandigarh State Commission report, LiveLaw,
https://www.livelaw.in/amp/consumer-cases/restaurants-cannot-charge-above-mrp-for-packaged-water-chandigarh-state-commission-514306 ,
and a Punjab State Commission report on LiveLaw.

## 4. "Chilled or not" [VERIFIED-PRIMARY application + SECONDARY support]
Rule 18(2) prohibits any sale of a packed commodity above its retail sale price and contains no exception for
cooling or refrigeration. Rule 2(m) makes the MRP the maximum price "inclusive of all taxes". So a shop selling the
sealed bottle over the counter can't add a "cooling charge" above the MRP. Supporting reports (SECONDARY): Andaman &
Nicobar Department of Civil Supplies & Consumer Affairs warnings against charging above MRP on cold drinks/water
(andamansheekha.com, nicobartimes.com) and consumer-forum orders on "cooling charges".
→ **CHECKPOINT B**: no national DoCA circular specifically on "cooling charges" was found. The line rests on the rule text.
