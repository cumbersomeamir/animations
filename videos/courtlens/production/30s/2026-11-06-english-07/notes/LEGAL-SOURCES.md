# Legal sources: "ATM debited your account but gave no cash: can the bank keep it?" (India)

Research date: 2026-11-06 (batch 2026-11-06-english-07). Language of the video: English.

## Topic choice
- The suggested subject was "can a shop charge more than the printed MRP for a packaged product". That is already
  Episode 02 (`context/episodes/2026-11-03-mrp-packaged-water.md`, round-02). As instructed, a fresh subject in the
  same genre was chosen instead.
- Scope: a domestic ATM withdrawal where the account is debited but no cash is dispensed.
- The example is fictional: ₹2,000 debited, balance from ₹12,450 to ₹10,450, card ••4821. In the example the money
  comes back 3 days after the T+5 limit, so ₹300 is owed. This is general information, not advice on any one case.

## 1. RBI "TAT" directions for failed transactions [VERIFIED-PRIMARY]
- RBI/2019-20/67, DPSS.CO.PD No.629/02.01.014/2019-20, **September 20, 2019**, "Harmonisation of Turn Around Time
  (TAT) and customer compensation for failed transactions using authorised Payment Systems". Issued under s.10(2)
  read with s.18 of the Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007. Effective October 15, 2019.
- Page: https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=11693&Mode=0 (read 2026-11-06). The live page now
  cites "Reserve Bank - Integrated Ombudsman Scheme, 2021 (as amended from time to time)".
  PDF: https://rbidocs.rbi.org.in/rdocs/notification/PDFs/CIRCULAR677EC931A7A65E4D99AA957D8E85BC0A2A.PDF
- **Para 5 (verbatim):** "Wherever financial compensation is involved, the same shall be effected to the customer's
  account suo moto, without waiting for a complaint or claim from the customer."
- **Para 4:** "the prescribed TAT is the outer limit for resolution of failed transactions"
- **Annex, general instruction 2:** a failed transaction is one "not fully completed due to any reason not
  attributable to the customer such as … non-availability of cash in an ATM …"
- **Annex, general instruction 4:** "T is the day of transaction and refers to the calendar date."
- **Annex row 1(a), ATMs including Micro-ATMs (verbatim):** "Customer's account debited but cash not dispensed." →
  "Pro-active reversal (R) of failed transaction within a maximum of T + 5 days." → "₹ 100/- per day of delay beyond
  T + 5 days, to the credit of the account holder."
- **Still in force (checked 2026-11-06):**
  - RBI's withdrawn-circular lists, including the 28 Nov 2025 consolidation that withdrew about 9,445 circulars, were
    searched. Neither the circular number nor its title appears:
    - https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/NotificationUserWithdrawnCircular.aspx
    - …WithdrawnCircularDOR.aspx
    - …WithdrawnCircularDOS.aspx
  - RBI's July 2025 ATM FAQ (§2) restates the rule.
- Narration uses: "must be credited back within five days", "a hundred rupees a day, without you asking", and the
  example "three days late? That's three hundred rupees".

## 2. RBI FAQ: ATM / White Label ATM (updated July 04, 2025) [VERIFIED-OFFICIAL]
- https://www.rbi.org.in/commonman/Upload/English/FAQs/PDFs/FAQATM04072025.pdf
  (sha256 `1afd706aad2dd70d82bd0a94aed779efa4cff153be123fa08bb66c0eb75ca35d`)
- **Q20:** "In the case of a failed ATM transaction, the banks have been mandated to re-credit the customer's account
  within a maximum of T+5 calendar days (where 'T' is the day of transaction)."
- **Q21:** "the card issuing bank has to pay compensation of Rs. 100/- per day for delay in re-crediting the
  customer's amount beyond 5 calendar days from the date of the failed ATM transaction. The compensation has to be
  credited to the account of the customer without any claim being made by the customer."
- **Q18:** banks should reverse on their own, but "it is always a good practice to lodge a complaint with the card
  issuing bank or ATM owner bank at the earliest".
- **Q20 covers "a failed ATM / WLA transaction"**, so the rule applies at any bank's ATM or a white-label ATM. Q21
  puts the compensation on the **card-issuing bank**, which is the customer's own bank. Narration uses: "your bank
  owes you", "even at another bank's ATM".

## 3. Escalation: Reserve Bank - Integrated Ombudsman Scheme, 2026 [VERIFIED-OFFICIAL]
- RB-IOS 2026 is in force from **July 1, 2026** and replaces RB-IOS 2021. FAQ:
  https://www.rbi.org.in/commonman/Upload/English/FAQs/PDFs/RBIOS01072026.pdf
  (sha256 `db923b0fe96466e98510a151404c3022b5d8e3a48b51e304c142e354ba0dca66`)
- **FAQ 17 (verbatim):** "The complainant must first approach the concerned Regulated Entity. A complaint can be
  filed before the RBI Ombudsman if the complainant has not received any reply within 30 days or within the time
  specified by RBI, NPCI or Card Network guidelines, if any, whichever is higher, after the Regulated Entity received
  the complaint; or if the complainant is not satisfied with the reply/resolution provided by the Regulated Entity."
  Time limit: within 90 days from the date the applicable timeline expires, or from the bank's last communication,
  whichever is later.
- Free of cost. Online at **https://cms.rbi.org.in**. RBI Contact Centre toll-free 14448.
- Narration uses: "Complain to your bank, and after thirty days, to the RBI Ombudsman". On screen: "Give it 30
  days", "Then: RBI Ombudsman", and the envelope label "cms.rbi.org.in".
- Simplification: the narration gives the common path, which is no fix after 30 days. The notes record that an
  unsatisfactory reply also allows escalation, and the 90-day limit.
