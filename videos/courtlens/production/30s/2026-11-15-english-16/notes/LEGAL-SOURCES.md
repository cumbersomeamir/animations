# Legal sources: "Their city, or yours?": filing a consumer complaint yourself, where you live (India)

Research date: 2026-11-15 (episode english-16, batch 16). Language of the video: English.

- **Already covered:**

- **Chosen fresh topic (same genre and story criteria, the natural next step after "they refused"):** a faulty product,
  an unresponsive company in another state, and the question "must I fight them in their city?". The answer rests on
  the Consumer Protection Act 2019 and its rules: where you can file, that you can file yourself, the deadline, the
  remedies, and the first step.
- **History check:** no earlier episode covers consumer-commission jurisdiction, self-filing, e-filing or limitation.
- **Jurisdiction:** all of India (central Act, central rules, NCDRC regulations).

## Fact classification
- **Statutory and regulatory facts:** quoted below.
- **Illustrative assumptions:**
  - the faulty phone
  - "no reply"
  - the company being in another state
  - the HQ tower and the home
  - the generic filing form: it shows the portal's name only and is not a copy of its interface
- **Practical suggestion:** "Keep your bill and every reply" (evidence). The commission decides on the evidence
  (s.38).
- **Simplifications (accurate for the depicted case, qualified here):**
  - "where you live or work": s.34(2)(d) says "resides or personally works for gain". The other venues in s.34(2)(a)–(c)
    remain available (where the opposite party is, or where the cause of action arose).
  - "district commission": the District Commission takes complaints where the consideration paid does not exceed
    ₹50 lakh (2021 Rules). A phone purchase fits; larger purchases go to the State or National Commission.
  - "two years from when the problem arose": s.69(1) says "from the date on which the cause of action has arisen". A
    later complaint can still be admitted if delay is condoned for sufficient cause, with reasons recorded (s.69(2)).
  - "a repair, replacement, refund or compensation": s.39(1)(a) "remove the defect" (rendered as "repair"), (b)
    replace, (c) return the price with interest, (d) compensation. The commission can also give other directions not
    narrated (e.g. discontinue an unfair trade practice).
  - "A lawyer is optional": parties may appear "in person or through agent or through counsel" (reg. 3(4)).
  - **Fee, in notes only:** nil for consideration up to ₹5 lakh, under the CDRC Rules 2020 rule 7(2) Table as
    substituted by the 2023 amendment (17 Aug 2023; reported as G.S.R. 606(E)). This is from a legal-database
    reproduction (legitquest). The official e-Gazette returned HTTP 503 and failed TLS from here, so the fee is not
    narrated.

## 1. Consumer Protection Act, 2019 (Act 35 of 2019)
- Official PDF (Department of Consumer Affairs): https://consumeraffairs.gov.in/public/upload/files/CP%20Act%202019_1732700731.pdf
  (sha256 `0ae7f82ab5b77d50087cd4cd2dd2dbc12755c0bdb31371fdefd042b020d50756`; the same official copy used in episode 04)
- **s.34(2):** "A complaint shall be instituted in a District Commission within the local limits of whose
  jurisdiction,— (a) the opposite party ... ordinarily resides or carries on business or has a branch office or
  personally works for gain; or (b) any of the opposite parties ...; or (c) the cause of action, wholly or in part,
  arises; or **(d) the complainant resides or personally works for gain.**"
- **s.35(1):** "A complaint ... may be filed with a District Commission by— **(a) the consumer**, (i) to whom such goods
  are sold or delivered ...; or (ii) who alleges unfair trade practice ...; ... **Provided that the complaint under
  this sub-section may be filed electronically in such manner as may be prescribed.**"
- **s.35(2):** "Every complaint ... shall be accompanied with such fee and payable in such manner, including
  electronic form, as may be prescribed."
- **s.39(1):** where the goods suffer from defects, the District Commission may direct the opposite party "(a) to
  remove the defect ...; (b) to replace the goods with new goods of similar description which shall be free from any
  defect; (c) to return to the complainant the price, or, as the case may be, the charges paid by the complainant
  along with such interest ...; (d) to pay such amount as may be awarded by it as compensation to the consumer ..."
- **s.69(1):** "The District Commission, the State Commission or the National Commission shall not admit a complaint
  unless it is filed within two years from the date on which the cause of action has arisen."
- **s.69(2):** delay may be condoned for "sufficient cause", with reasons recorded.

## 2. Consumer Protection (Jurisdiction of the District Commission, the State Commission and the National Commission) Rules, 2021
- G.S.R. 912(E), 30 Dec 2021.
- Copy hosted by the Tripura High Court:
  https://www.thc.nic.in/Central%20Governmental%20Rules/Consumer%20Protection%20(Jurisdiction%20of%20the%20District%20Commission,%20the%20State%20Commission%20and%20the%20National%20Commission)%20Rules,%202021.pdf
  (sha256 `2c10f5a312064d5d7eaf80181580919fc13aff09f2df47b4ecc2ca7b79ade979`)
- **Rule 3:** "the District Commission shall have jurisdiction to entertain complaints where the value of the goods or
  services paid as consideration does not exceed fifty lakh rupees."
- **Rule 4:** State Commission, above ₹50 lakh up to ₹2 crore.
- **Rule 5:** National Commission, above ₹2 crore.

## 3. Consumer Protection (Consumer Commission Procedure) Regulations, 2020 (NCDRC)
- F. No. A-105/CCPR/NCDRC/2020, 24 Jul 2020.
- Reproduction: https://taxguru.in/corporate-law/consumer-protection-consumer-commission-procedure-regulations-2020.html
- **Reg. 3(4):** "The complainant and the opposite party can appear in person or through agent or through counsel."

## 4. e-Jagriti: current status (official)
- **PIB "National Consumer Day" backgrounder, 23 Dec 2025:**
  https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/dec/doc20251223743001.pdf
  (sha256 `745c0c9ac3da56e64f0fe45b5eaa566e1aa36e7dd214da66511671bd5a9a75d1`)
  > "e-Jagriti, launched on 1 January 2025, ... integrates earlier systems, including OCMS, e-Daakhil, NCDRC CMS, and
  > CONFONET ... Now operational across the NCDRC and all States and Union Territories, the platform enables users to
  > file complaints, make payments, participate in virtual hearings, and monitor case progress."
  - The same document puts District Commission claims at up to ₹50 lakh.
- **PIB World Consumer Rights Day 2025 backgrounder (14 Mar 2025):**
  https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/mar/doc2025314520301.pdf
