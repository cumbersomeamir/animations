# Legal sources: "Can a landlord keep your deposit for normal wear and tear?" (Tamil Nadu, India)

Research date: 2026-11-18 (episode english-20, batch 20). Language of the video: English.

## Topic and jurisdiction
- **Topic history:** new. Episodes 11 (landlord power cut) and 12 (landlord entry) used the same Tamil Nadu Act, but
  no earlier episode covered the security deposit, its refund or wear and tear.
- **Jurisdiction: Tamil Nadu only.** India has no single all-India tenancy statute. Each state has its own law, and
  the Model Tenancy Act 2021 is only a model for states. The narration names Tamil Nadu, and a "TAMIL NADU / TENANCY
  ACT" stamp appears on screen. Nothing is presented as an all-India rule.
- **Extent:** s.1(2), "all urban areas of the State of Tamil Nadu". Exclusions in s.3: government-owned premises,
  employer accommodation, notified religious/charitable premises, Waqf/trust premises, notified exemptions. These limits
  stay in the notes. The video says "Tamil Nadu's tenancy law", which is accurate.
- **Commencement:** 22 Feb 2019 (G.O. (Ms) No. 36, Housing and Urban Development, 22 Feb 2019, per secondary sources
  and a contemporary DT Next report). The Act also repealed the TN Buildings (Lease and Rent Control) Act 1960.
- **Scene:** a fictional rented flat. The ₹45,000 deposit and the ₹1,500 window deduction are **illustrative amounts**,
  not legal figures.

## Provisions used (verbatim)
- **s.11, "Security deposit"** (as enacted):
  > (1) Save an agreement to the contrary, it shall be unlawful to charge a security deposit in excess of three times
  > the monthly rent.
  > (2) The security deposit shall be refunded to the tenant within one month after vacation of the premises, after
  > making due deduction of any liability of the tenant.
- **Act 39 of 2018, s.9** (assent 14 Jul 2018; no separate commencement clause; published before the principal Act
  commenced):
  > In section 11 of the principal Act, in sub-section (2), for the expression "within one month after vacation of the
  > premises", the expression "at the time of taking over of possession of the vacant premises by the landlord" shall be
  > substituted.

  So s.11(2) now reads: "refunded to the tenant at the time of taking over of possession of the vacant premises by the
  landlord, after making due deduction of any liability of the tenant." Contemporary news (DT Next, 23 Feb 2019)
  still quoted the original "within a month"; the video follows the amended Gazette text.
- **s.15(1), "Repair and maintenance of property":**
  > Notwithstanding any contract in writing to the contrary, the landlord and the tenant shall be bound to keep the
  > premises in as good a condition as at the commencement of the tenancy, except for normal wear and tear, and shall
  > be responsible for the respective repairs and maintenance as specified in the Second Schedule.
- **s.15(3) as substituted by Act 39 of 2018, s.13:** if the tenant refuses scheduled or agreed repairs, the landlord
  gets them done and the tenant must pay within one month of the landlord's notice. Notes only.
- **s.16, "Tenant to look after the premises":**
  > During the tenancy, the tenant shall— (a) not intentionally or negligently damage the premises or permit such
  > damage; (b) notify the landlord of any damage as soon as possible; (c) take reasonable care of the premises and its
  > contents and keep it reasonably habitable having regard to its condition at the commencement of tenancy and the
  > normal incidence of living.
- **Second Schedule, Part A, "Responsibilities of the Landlord":**
  > 1. Structural repairs except those necessitated by damage caused by the tenant. 2. Whitewashing of walls and
  > painting of doors and windows. 3. Changing and plumbing pipes when necessary. 4. Internal and external electrical
  > wiring and related maintenance when necessary.
- **Second Schedule, Part B (tenant's periodic repairs), notes only:** tap washers and taps, drain cleaning, WC, wash
  basin, bath tub and geyser repairs, circuit breakers, switches and sockets, electrical equipment, kitchen fixtures,
  knobs and locks, fly nets, **"Replacement of glass panels in windows, doors etc."**, gardens.
- **Forum (notes only, not narrated):** s.34 (as renumbered in 2018) gives the Rent Court exclusive jurisdiction,
  except the Rent Authority's s.31 powers (ss.9, 10, 14, 15 and 20; "19 or 20" under the 2022 Act once notified). So a
  deposit-refund dispute under s.11 is for the Rent Court, and a repairs dispute under s.15 for the Rent Authority.
  s.4-A (2018) bars an unregistered tenancy document from being received in evidence. The video names no forum: it
  would have needed the registration caveat, and the 18 Nov feedback asked for less information on screen.
- **Later amendments checked:** Act 22 of 2019 (s.4 deadlines, extension to other areas), Act 3 of 2020 and Act 19 of
  2022. Act 19 of 2022 adds s.15(6) (force majeure; refund within 15 days if the premises cannot be made habitable) and
  comes into force on a notified date, which was not confirmed. None changes s.11(2), s.15(1), s.16 or the Second
  Schedule. Web searches on 2026-11-18 found no 2023–2026 amendment Act.

## Fact classification
- **Statutory facts:** ss.11(2), 15(1), 16(a) and Second Schedule Part A item 2, as quoted.
- **Interpretation (stated plainly here):** a liability the Act excludes ("except for normal wear and tear") or places
  on the landlord (repainting) is not a "liability of the tenant" that s.11(2) lets the landlord deduct. No court ruling
  on this point was read for this episode, and the video makes no claim about one.
- **Illustrative assumptions:** the flat, ₹45,000, the cricket ball, ₹1,500 for the window, the agreement's contrary
  line.
- **Practical suggestion, not a statutory duty:** photos at move-in and move-out; asking for deductions in writing.

## Not claimed
- No all-India rule. Nothing is said about states other than Tamil Nadu.
- No interest or penalty for a late refund. A commentary claimed 8% interest, but no such text was found in the Act,
  so it is not used.
- No promised outcome. No forum is named, and the deposit cap (three months' rent, "save an agreement to the contrary")
  is not mentioned.
