# Legal sources: "Pay early, get fined?" Can a lender charge you for paying off a loan early? (India)

Research date: 2026-11-18 (episode english-21, batch 21). Language of the video: English.

## Topic decision (why not the proposed topic)
- **Proposed:** "Can a shop refuse a return simply because you changed your mind in India? Contrast a faulty product
  with change-of-mind and a disclosed policy… one relatable jacket receipt."
- **Already covered.** That exact contrast was the central split of two delivered episodes:
  - **Episode 01** (2026-11-02, English, online): "Changing your mind is a different situation; there is no automatic
    right to return every purchase." The split was "RULE APPLIES" (defective / not as described) vs "NO AUTOMATIC
    RIGHT – depends on the return policy".
  - **Episode 04** (2026-11-04, Hindi, physical shop): "मन बदला हो, तो वापसी दुकान की नीति पर है" ("if you changed your
    mind, a return depends on the shop's policy"), with a defective vs change-of-mind split.
  - The same contrast was rejected as a repeat in batches 08 ("changed my mind vs defective"), 13 and 16 ("NO RETURNS
    vs defective").
- **Chosen instead (same genre: one everyday money situation, a conditional answer from the official rule):**
  pre-payment charges on loans. No earlier episode (01–20) covered loans. The rule is current (consolidated
  Nov 2025, updated through Oct 2026) and gives a clear, conditional, non-universal answer. Floating-rate personal
  loans carry no charge; fixed-rate loans may carry one if it is disclosed.

## Provisions used (verbatim)
- **Banks, para 352** (existing loans):
  > In case of existing floating rate term loans sanctioned before December 31, 2025 for purposes other than business to
  > individual borrowers with or without co-obligant(s), a bank shall not charge pre-payment charges.
- **Banks, para 353** (loans sanctioned or renewed on or after 1 Jan 2026):
  > (1) For all floating rate loans granted for purposes other than business to individuals, with or without
  > co-obligant(s), a bank shall not levy pre-payment charges
  > (2) For all floating rate loans granted for business purpose to individuals and MSEs, with or without
  > co-obligant(s), a bank shall not levy any pre-payment charges.
  > (3) … applicable irrespective of the source of funds used for pre-payment of loans, either in part or in full, and
  > without any minimum lock-in period.
  > (4) Applicability … for dual / special rate … will depend on whether the loan is on floating rate at the time of
  > pre-payment.
  > (5) In cases other than those mentioned at paragraphs 353(1) and 353(2) above, pre-payment charges, if any, shall be
  > as per the approved policy of the bank. …
  > (8) The applicability or otherwise of pre-payment charges shall be clearly disclosed in the sanction letter and loan
  > agreement. Further, in case of loans and advances where KFS is to be provided …, the same shall also be mentioned
  > in the KFS. No pre-payment charges which have not been disclosed as specified herein shall be charged by the bank.
- **NBFCs, para 33** (existing loans):
  > In case of existing loans sanctioned or renewed on or before December 31, 2025, an NBFC shall not levy pre-payment
  > charges on any floating rate term loan sanctioned for purposes other than business to individual borrowers, with or
  > without co-obligant(s).
- **NBFCs, para 34** (from 1 Jan 2026): (1) no charges on all floating-rate non-business loans to individuals; (2)
  business loans to individuals/MSEs: none for NBFC-Upper Layer, none up to ₹50 lakh for NBFC-Middle Layer; (3)
  source of funds, part or full, no lock-in; (4) dual rate; (5) other cases per policy; (8) disclosure in the sanction
  letter, agreement and KFS, with no undisclosed charge.

## Scope notes (kept out of the narration to limit on-screen information)
1. **Loans sanctioned before 1 Jan 2026:** paras 352 and 33 ban pre-payment charges on floating-rate *term* loans to
   individuals for non-business purposes, without exceptions. The explicit "source of funds / part or full / no
   lock-in" wording sits in 353(3) and 34(3) for loans from 1 Jan 2026. The narration's "part or full, even with money
   from a new lender" is literally in the new-loan text and is consistent with the unconditional older ban. No
   older-loan carve-out was found.
2. **Fixed rate:** for loans from 1 Jan 2026, a charge must be disclosed in the sanction letter and agreement (and the
   KFS) or it can't be charged. For older fixed-rate loans the narration says only that "a charge may apply, if your
   loan papers disclose it". It makes no claim that every older undisclosed charge is barred.
3. **Business loans:** commercial banks may not charge on floating-rate business loans to individuals and MSEs either
   (353(2)). NBFC rules depend on the layer. The video doesn't say business loans are charged; it shows only the
   non-business condition, with no ✕ on business.
4. **Dual-rate loans:** depend on whether the loan is floating at the time of pre-payment (353(4)/34(4)). Notes only.
5. **Lenders not verified individually:** Small Finance Banks, Payments Banks, RRBs, Local Area Banks and co-operative
   banks have separate consolidated directions, which were not read. The July 2025 Directions applied para 5(i) to
   all commercial banks except payments banks, and to co-operative banks.

## Fact classification
- **Regulatory facts:** the paras above, read on rbi.org.in on 2026-11-18.
- **Illustrative:** the 2% charge, the cash, the "LOAN" ball and chain, the debt tower, the "new lender" building and
  the sanction-letter layout (a generic illustration, not a real lender's form).
- **Practical suggestion:** check the rate type in the sanction letter before prepaying.

## Not claimed
- No claim that every loan is free to prepay, and no universal right.
- No claim about compensation or refunds for charges already paid, and no forum is named.
