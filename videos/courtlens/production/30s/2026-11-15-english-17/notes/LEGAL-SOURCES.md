# Legal sources: "30 grams missing. Legal?": short net quantity in a sealed pack (India)

Research date: 2026-11-15 (episode english-17, batch 17). Language of the video: English.

## Topic decision
- **Proposed:** "Can a delivery app charge more than the printed MRP?" (sealed-goods MRP vs separately disclosed
  delivery/service fees; one giant packet and invoice line items; hook "THE EXTRA RUPEE").
- **Overlap found in the completed history:**
  - Episode 02 (2026-11-03) taught this rule: no sale of a packed commodity above its retail sale price (Packaged
    Commodities Rules r.18(2)); MRP is inclusive of all taxes (r.2(m)); restaurants and hotels serving you are "a
    different case" (Supreme Court, CA 21790/2017).
  - Episode 05 covered charges added at checkout under the CCPA dark-patterns guidelines (basket sneaking).
  - A delivery-app MRP episode would repeat Episode 02's core price rule, with the fee angle overlapping Episode 05's
    checkout instrument.
- **Chosen (same genre and story criteria: one giant sealed packet, one precise rule, what it does and does not
  cover):** the *quantity* promise on the same kind of pack. A sealed "NET QTY 1 kg" pack holds 970 g. Is that legal?
  The Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules answer it precisely:
  - the maximum permissible error for a 1 kg pack (15 g)
  - what happens on a consumer complaint (test at the shop; seizure if short beyond the error)
  - the "when packed" exception
- **History check:** no earlier episode covers net quantity or permissible error. Episode 02 used only r.18(2), r.2(m)
  and r.6(1)(e) (MRP).
- **Jurisdiction:** all of India (central rules under the Legal Metrology Act, 2009). Enforcement is by the Director
  (Centre) and the Controllers / Legal Metrology Officers (states).

## Fact classification
- **Regulatory facts:** quoted below.
- **Illustrative assumptions:**
  - the rice pack, the bowl and the 970 g reading
  - the officer badge (generic, no real insignia)
  - the shop counter
  - the "−30 g" tag
  - The arithmetic follows: 30 g short = twice the 15 g error.
- **Practical suggestion:** "Weigh just the contents, keep the bill, and complain to Legal Metrology". The declared
  quantity is the net quantity of the commodity in the package (r.6(1)(c)), and r.21(1)(i) is triggered by a
  purchaser's complaint.
- **Qualifications (in notes; the video keeps one concise caveat):**
  - The 15 g error is for a 500–1000 g declared quantity (Table I row vi). Row vii (1000–10000 g, 1.5%) gives the same
    15 g at exactly 1 kg. Other sizes have other limits (e.g. 3% for 300–500 g).
  - Within the error the pack may be sold (r.21(4)). Beyond it, the officer "shall seize such package and take
    appropriate action against the retail dealer or the wholesale dealer" (r.21(3)).
  - **"When packed" proviso (r.21(3)):** no punitive action against the retail or wholesale dealer if the officer is
    satisfied after testing that the deficiency is due to environmental conditions. The video says "can be excused".
  - Manufacturer-level checks are statistical (r.19–20 as amended 2017: corrected average, Fifth Schedule sample
    sizes, no package short by more than twice the error). This is not narrated.
  - The penalty amounts (Legal Metrology Act and r.32) are not narrated.

## 1. Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules, 2011 (G.S.R. 202(E))
- **Source:** principal Gazette scan from the Department of Consumer Affairs:
  https://consumeraffairs.gov.in/public/upload/files/8_1732871406.pdf
  - sha256 `48cd6acdb5475709631b741d71b2948d45713e7e8758ce531c19948a7e31f890`; the same file verified for episode 02
  - The scan has no text layer; pages were rendered and read visually: p.43 r.6(1)(c), pp.63–64 r.21, p.72 First
    Schedule.
- **Rule 6(1)(c)** (p.43): "The net quantity, in terms of the standard unit of weight or measure, of the commodity
  contained in the package ... shall be mentioned."
- **Rule 21 "Inspection of quantity and error in packages at the premises of the wholesale dealer or retail dealer"**
  (pp.63–64):
  - **(1)** Ordinarily no test at the retailer's premises "unless— (i) a complaint is received by the Director or the
    Controller or any Legal Metrology Officer to the effect that the package sold or delivered to the complainant does
    not contain the quantity declared on such package ..."
  - **(2)** The officer verifies the quantity and "whether the deficiency is more than the maximum permissible error in
    relation to that commodity."
  - **(3)** If the error in deficiency "is more than the maximum permissible error in relation to that commodity, he
    shall seize such package and take appropriate action against the retail dealer or the wholesale dealer ...:
    Provided that where the package bears the legend 'when packed', no punitive action shall be taken against the
    retail dealer or wholesale dealer if the Director, Controller or any Legal Metrology Officer is satisfied after
    necessary test that the deficiency in the net quantity contained in package is due to environmental conditions."
  - **(4)** Where the deficiency "is not more than the maximum permissible error, the retail dealer shall be at
    liberty to sell ..."
- **First Schedule, para 1, Table I** (p.72), maximum permissible errors on net quantities declared by weight or
  volume:
  - up to 50 g: 9%
  - 50–100: 4.5 g
  - 100–200: 4.5%
  - 200–300: 9 g
  - 300–500: 3%
  - **500–1000: 15 g**
  - 1000–10000: 1.5%
  - 10000–15000: 150 g
  - more than 15000: 1.0%
  - Para 1(1): "in excess or in deficiency".
