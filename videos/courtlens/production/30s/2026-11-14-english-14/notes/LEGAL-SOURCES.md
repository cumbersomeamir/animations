# Legal sources: "Can the electricity company cut your power over a bill you dispute?" (India)

Research date: 2026-11-14 (episode english-14). Language of the video: English.

## Topic decision
- **Proposed:** "Can a restaurant legally force you to pay its service charge in India?" The brief asked for one bill
  and one disputed line, service charge vs GST vs tips, the current status, and a practical takeaway.
- **Already covered:** Episode 06 (2026-11-06, English, `context/episodes/2026-11-06-english06-service-charge.md`)
  covered exactly this:
  - the CCPA guidelines of 4 Jul 2022
  - the Delhi High Court ruling of 28 Mar 2025 upholding them
  - service charge vs tip vs GST, on a ₹1,000 bill
  - "Refused? Keep the bill and call 1915"
  Repeating it would duplicate the subject, so a genuinely new topic in the same genre was chosen.
- **Chosen topic (same story criteria):** "one bill and one disputed line" is kept literally. A household electricity
  bill shows one disputed month, and the question is whether the supplier can disconnect over it. Payoff: the
  statutory notice, the under-protest deposit, and a practical first step.
- **History check:**
  - No earlier Courtlens episode covers electricity billing or disconnection by a supplier.
  - Episode 11 (Tamil Nadu) was about a *landlord* cutting a tenant's power to force them out, under the state tenancy
    Act, s.20. This episode is about the *licensee* (discom) under the central Electricity Act — a different actor,
    law and remedy.
- **Jurisdiction:** all of India. The Electricity Act, 2003 is a central Act. State commissions add procedure
  (supply codes, grievance forums) but do not displace s.56.

## Fact classification
- **Statutory facts (quoted below):**
  - written notice of at least 15 clear days before disconnection for non-payment
  - in a dispute, depositing under protest the lower of (a) the sum claimed or (b) the charges for each month at the
    preceding six months' average stops the cut-off pending the dispute
- **Illustrative assumptions (fictional, not measured):**
  - the bill: last month ₹1,400, this month ₹9,800
  - the six monthly bars (₹1,320 / 1,480 / 1,350 / 1,460 / 1,390 / 1,400, average ₹1,400)
  - one disputed month
  - the "pay all or disconnect" notice
  - the lamp and the plug
- **Practical suggestions, not statutory duties:**
  - "Put your dispute in writing" gives a record that a dispute exists, which the proviso needs ("pending disposal of
    any dispute").
  - "Keep the receipt" is proof of the deposit under protest.
- **Qualifications kept in these notes, not the video:**
  - Theft and unauthorised-use cases (Electricity Act ss.126 and 135) follow separate procedures and are outside this
    episode.
  - s.56(2) bars recovery of sums older than two years unless shown continuously as arrears. It is true but omitted
    to keep one idea per beat.
  - Escalation forum: each distribution licensee must run a consumer grievance redressal forum (s.42(5)), with a state
    Ombudsman above it (s.42(6)). Not narrated.

## 1. Electricity Act, 2003, s.56 (verbatim)
- **Source:** the Supreme Court's reproduction of the section in *Bihar State Electricity Board etc. v. M/s Iceberg
  Industries Ltd. and others*, Civil Appeal Nos. 7649–7651 of 2019, judgment of 27 April 2020 (Aniruddha Bose J.),
  paragraph 18.
  - PDF: https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2013/16858/16858_2013_32_1507_21869_Judgement_27-Apr-2020.pdf
  - sha256 `68bc00d2a9d0349aa47b7880e2527716e854cf41f67db9fff61a4dc2d1e4b2a8`; read with pdftotext and kept in the
    session scratchpad, not committed.
  - The official India Code copy (https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/2058/1/a200336.pdf) returned a CDN
    error page, and its handle page returned 403, from this environment.
- **s.56:**
  > "56 Disconnection of supply in default of payment- (1) Where any person neglects to pay any charge for electricity
  > or any sum other than a charge for electricity due from him to a licensee or the generating company in respect of
  > supply, transmission or distribution or wheeling of electricity to him, the licensee or the generating company may,
  > after giving not less than fifteen clear days' notice in writing, to such person and without prejudice to his
  > rights to recover such charge or other sum by suit, cut off the supply of electricity ... and may discontinue the
  > supply until such charge or other sum, together with any expenses incurred by him in cutting off and reconnecting
  > the supply, are paid, but no longer:
  > Provided that the supply of electricity shall not be cut off if such person deposits, under protest,-
  > (a) an amount equal to the sum claimed from him, or
  > (b) the electricity charges due from him for each month calculated on the basis of average charge for electricity
  > paid by him during the preceding six months,
  > whichever is less, pending disposal of any dispute between him and the licensee.
  > (2) Notwithstanding anything contained in any other law for the time being in force, no sum due from any consumer,
  > under this section shall be recoverable after the period of two years from the date when such sum became first due
  > unless such sum has been shown continuously as recoverable as arrear of charges for electricity supplied and the
  > licensee shall not cut off the supply of the electricity."
- **Para 19 of the same judgment:** "disconnection of supply is special power given to the supplier in addition to the
  normal mode of recovery by instituting a suit."

## 2. Current status check (as of 14 Nov 2026)
- **Draft Electricity (Amendment) Bill, 2025:** the Ministry of Power released it for comments on 9 Oct 2025.
  - The PRS Legislative Brief (https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/bills_parliament/2025/Brief_Draft-Electricity_(A)_Bill_2025.pdf)
    covers network sharing, cross-subsidy, tariffs and the Electricity Council. It does not mention s.56.
  - IIT Kanpur's clause-by-clause note on the draft
    (https://cer.iitk.ac.in/odf_assets/upload_files/blog_Seeking_comments_on_Draft_Electricity_Amendment_Bill_20253.pdf;
    sha256 `b10ca133c95a2416b6123ad43215ee757499ec5c992ead63c8b3659319c4421f`) contains no amendment to s.56.
  - The Ministry's own PDF returned 403 from this environment.
- **Enactment:**
  - No evidence of enactment was found. News up to March 2026 described the bill as still to be tabled.
  - The PRS bill-track page shows a "Passed" label but describes the bill only as a draft; this conflict is
    unexplained.
  - Either way, the draft as published does not amend s.56. The section is presented as current law; this is a
    checkpoint to re-verify if the bill is enacted.
- The Electricity (Amendment) Rules, 2026 (captive generation, Rule 3 of the Electricity Rules 2005) do not touch s.56.

## 3. Electricity Act, 2003, s.42(5)–(6): grievance forum and Ombudsman (notes only)
- Secondary reproduction: https://www.advocatekhoj.com/library/bareActs/electricity/42.php
  - (5) every distribution licensee shall "establish a forum for redressal of grievances of the consumers in
    accordance with the guidelines as may be specified by the State Commission"
  - (6) a consumer "aggrieved by non-redressal of his grievances under sub-section (5), may make a representation ...
    to an authority to be known as Ombudsman to be appointed or designated by the State Commission"
