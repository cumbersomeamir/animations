# Legal sources: "क्या अपनी कॉल रिकॉर्ड करना भारत में कानूनी है?" (Recording a call you are part of, INDIA)

Research date: 2026-11-03. Language of the video: Hindi. Scope: an ordinary person recording a phone call they are
taking part in (here: a landlord promising to return a deposit).
Status key: **VERIFIED-PRIMARY** = judgment text from the Supreme Court's own host; **VERIFIED-TEXT** = full judgment
text read on a reputable reproduction (official host unreachable); **CHECKPOINT** = open item.

## Why the claim is narrowed (no blanket yes/no)
No source found for this episode says, in one rule, that recording your own call is lawful or unlawful in every
context. The Supreme Court material decides **admissibility as evidence**, and separately recognises a **privacy
action for publishing** private matters. Lower courts have taken different views on privacy in covert spousal
recordings (e.g. the Punjab & Haryana High Court order reversed in *Vibhor Garg*). So the video:
1. asks "क्या यह कानूनी है?" but answers only what is supported, namely that a covert recording **can become
   evidence** if the three-fold test is met;
2. separates **posting it online**, where a damages claim for privacy is possible;
3. gives one practical step: keep the original file unedited.
It never says "recording is legal" or "illegal".

## 1. Evidence: covert recordings can be admitted [VERIFIED-PRIMARY]
**Vibhor Garg v. Neha**, 2025 INSC 829, Supreme Court of India, judgment of **14 July 2025** (B.V. Nagarathna and
Satish Chandra Sharma, JJ.), arising from SLP (C) 21195/2021.
PDF: https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2021/31421/31421_2021_5_1501_62289_Judgement_14-Jul-2025.pdf
(sha256 `e6c41f5420cb2010ec0ea882effd4d2ad26fb4b34d4893f60f11e6fde622ef67`)

Para 9.5:
> The three-fold test of relevance, identification and accuracy has to be satisfied before a Court admits a recorded
> conversation in evidence. However, the fact that the conversation was recorded without the consent and knowledge
> of the person speaking is not a prohibition on the admissibility of the evidence…

Para 13: the Family Court was directed to take the memory cards, CD and transcript on record "and consider the same
as evidence, in accordance with law." The case concerned spouses (Evidence Act s.122). Para 9.5 states the general
test for recorded conversations.

**R.M. Malkani v. State of Maharashtra**, (1973) 2 SCR 417, judgment of 22 Sep 1972. Official PDF:
https://api.sci.gov.in/jonew/judis/6708.pdf (sha256 `0a99c68e…9abc`). Quoted in *Vibhor Garg* para 9.4:
> Tape recorded conversation is admissible provided first the conversation is relevant to the matters in issue;
> secondly, there is identification of the voice; and thirdly, the accuracy of the tape recorded conversation is
> proved by eliminating the possibility of erasing the tape record.

Context only, not on screen: Malkani also held that where a person on the phone allows another to record it, there
was "no violation of the Indian Telegraph Act". That Act is now replaced by the Telecommunications Act, 2023, which
this episode does not interpret.

On-screen mapping:
- the three chips "मामले की बात / आवाज़ की पहचान / कोई बदलाव नहीं" are relevance / identification / accuracy
  (no erasure or tampering);
- the action "मूल फ़ाइल बिना एडिट किए रखें" follows from the accuracy limb.

## 2. Posting online is a different question [VERIFIED-TEXT]
**R. Rajagopal v. State of Tamil Nadu**, (1994) 6 SCC 632, judgment of **7 October 1994**, para 26(1). Full text read
at https://indiankanoon.org/doc/501107/ ; the official SCR PDF on digiscr.sci.gov.in returned HTTP 502 to this container.
→ **CHECKPOINT A**: re-read the official copy.
> A citizen has a right to safeguard the privacy of his own, his family, marriage, procreation, motherhood,
> child-bearing and education among other matters. None can publish anything concerning the above matters without
> his consent whether truthful or otherwise and whether laudatory or critical. If he does so, he would be violating
> the right to privacy of the person concerned and would be liable in an action for damages. Position may, however,
> be different, if a person voluntarily thrusts himself into controversy…

Para 26(2) excepts publications based on public records, among other exceptions. The narration therefore says only
that spreading private matters without consent "can" bring a damages claim. The on-screen sticker reads
"सुप्रीम कोर्ट 1994".
