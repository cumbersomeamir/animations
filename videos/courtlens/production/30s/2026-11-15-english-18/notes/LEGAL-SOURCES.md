# Legal sources: "Your snack. Their rule?": can a cinema stop you bringing your own food? (India)

Research date: 2026-11-15 (episode english-18, batch 18). Language of the video: English.

## Topic decision
- **Proposed topic, used as given.** Episode history (01–17) has no cinema, entry-condition or outside-food episode.
  The nearest are 02 (above-MRP water; it mentioned restaurants as "a different case") and 06 (restaurant service
  charge). Both are about pricing, not entry conditions.
- **Jurisdiction:** all of India (a Supreme Court judgment under Article 141). The appeals came from Jammu & Kashmir.
  The Court also dismissed the transferred writ petitions from the Delhi and Bombay High Courts on the same issue.
- **Freshness check (15 Nov 2026):** a web search found no later statute or ruling changing the position. There are
  press reports of state statements (e.g. Maharashtra) and consumer-commission orders on water, but no confirmed
  2025–26 rule.
- **Scope note:** the Court's reasoning applies "absent a statutory regulation" (para 28). A state could legislate
  differently. This is in the notes, not narrated.

## Fact classification
- **Judicial holding:** cinemas may set entry conditions, including no outside food (paras 23, 32, 35).
- **Recorded statements, not court directions:** the cinemas' counsel stated that free hygienic drinking water is
  provided and that reasonable food for an infant or young child is allowed in practice (para 33). The video
  attributes these to the cinemas: "cinemas told the court", "they also said".
- **Court request, not an order:** consider medical/dietary cases case by case (para 34). Video: "The court asked
  cinemas to consider it."
- **Illustrative:** the snack bag, the cinema front, the sign, the glass, the bottle and lunchbox, the doctor's note.
  All are generic, with no real cinema chain or brand.
- **Practical suggestions:** ask for the free water; pack a reasonable amount of your child's food; carry a doctor's
  note and ask the manager. These follow from paras 33–34; none is a guaranteed entitlement.

## Supreme Court of India: K C Cinema (Correct Name K C Theatre) v. State of Jammu and Kashmir & Ors
- **Case:** Civil Appeal No 77 of 2023 (with CA 78, 79 of 2023; TC (C) 28 and 29 of 2019), decided **3 January 2023**.
  Dr Dhananjaya Y Chandrachud CJI and Pamidighantam Sri Narasimha J.
- **Official PDF:** https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2018/28193/28193_2018_1_42_40694_Judgement_03-Jan-2023.pdf
  (sha256 `c9a7ddac34b814fb3fa160db745117f06d37de2fa69746455c465287a487e384`; text extracted with pdftotext).
- **Para 23:** "The cinema hall is a private property of the owner of the hall. The owner of the hall is entitled to
  stipulate terms and conditions so long as they are not contrary to public interest, safety and welfare."
- **Para 32:** "... movie goers are bound by the condition of entry determined by the theatre owners in the instant case
  i.e., the prohibition on carrying food and beverages from outside into the precincts of the movie hall."
- **Para 33:** "We note that during the course of arguments, statements were made on behalf of the appellants that in
  order to obviate any inconvenience to movie goers, due arrangements are made for the supply of hygienic drinking
  water without levying any charge within the precincts of the cinema hall. Moreover, it has also been stated that
  when an infant or young child accompanies a parent, as a matter of practice, the cinema hall owners have no
  objection to a reasonable amount of food or beverages being carried inside the cinema hall to serve the
  nutritional requirements of the infant or child."
- **Para 34:** "As for movie goers with chronic diseases who may have received dietary instructions from their doctors
  or who may otherwise be under dietary restrictions due to their medical condition, we request cinema hall owners to
  consider requests from such movie goers on a case-by-case basis."
- **Para 35:** the appeals were allowed and the J&K High Court's direction (i) was set aside. That direction read:
  "Multiplexes/Cinema Halls Owners of the State of J&K are directed not to prohibit cinema goers/viewers henceforth
  from carrying his/her own food articles and water inside the theatre".
- **Para 29 (limit, notes only):** terms imposed by businesses do not bind consumers "in every case". Unreasonable
  terms against a party with lower bargaining power may not be enforced.
- **Para 40:** the transferred writ petitions from the Delhi and Bombay High Courts were dismissed.
