# Courtlens approved feedback rules (from "Published apps", Satisfied? column, read 11 Nov 2026) [USER DIRECTIVE]

These rules apply to every Courtlens educational episode from english-12 onward. Where they conflict with older
sections of `08-series-format.md` (layout, caption band, scene density), **these rules win**. Genre, palette, icon,
narrator voice and the "Is this legal in India?" identity stay as in `08-series-format.md`.

## Feedback on recent episodes
- Dense text, too many facts at once, weak motion, weak thumbnail.
- Keep the "Is this legal in India?" genre and the current visual identity.
- Use one central visual (e.g. tenant / door / key), then reveal the relevant condition. No wall of law text.

## Story structure ("Theory of Marketing")
- Thumbnail → hook → optional super hook → build-up + proof → payoff → CTA.
- The **hook** answers "why should I care". The **build-up** sustains one specific question. **Proof** makes the
  answer credible. The **payoff** gives useful knowledge. The **CTA** gives a relevant next step.
- Suspense must come from a real unanswered question, never a false claim.
- Don't repeat the answer in every scene, and don't pad with generic statements.

## Pacing (adapt naturally; always exactly 30.000 s)
| Window | Purpose |
|---|---|
| 0–3 s | immediate striking image + hook |
| 3–5 s | optional escalation |
| 5–21 s | visual explanation and proof, with scene changes |
| 21–27 s | resolve the question with a concrete takeaway |
| 27–30 s | concise, app-relevant CTA |

## Thumbnail
- A separate vertical thumbnail (1080x1920), tied to the hook, with **2–4 large words**. It is delivered with the MP4.

## Visual rules
- One visual idea at a time. No paragraph cards.
- On-screen labels are large and brief; the voice explains the detail.
- Captions must be readable within **x = 100..900, y = 230..1500**, avoiding platform overlays.
- Never show captions, diagram labels and an explanatory paragraph competing at the same time.
- Reveal diagrams sequentially. Use deliberate depth, lighting and transitions. Avoid decorative, distracting lines.
- Motion must be meaningful and explain the topic.

## Facts and claims
- Verify factual claims from primary or reliable sources before the final script. Distinguish measured facts,
  estimates and illustrative assumptions.
- Sources go in the project notes, not cluttering the video.
- Never invent prices, returns, news, app functionality or scientific certainty.
- No boilerplate warning/disclaimer narration. Omit risky procedures instead of building the story around warnings.
- Legal qualifications that are essential to the answer stay concise and accurate (for example, name the state when a
  rule is state-specific; never universalise one state's notice period to all of India).
- Check previous episodes to avoid repeating a topic. If the proposed topic was covered, choose a genuinely different
  one in the same genre. If a fresh-news claim can't be verified, choose another verified story; never present rumour
  as news.

## Delivery and QA
- Real 30.000 s 1080x1920 H.264/yuv420p MP4 with AAC, English voiceover and English captions; no Hindi.
- Keep the established narrator (Kokoro af_bella 0.7 + am_puck 0.3, en-us, 0.98). No Blender or paid APIs. Use the
  existing approved motion skill and assets.
- Don't overwrite earlier outputs. Only completed MP4s go to the user review folder (when downloaded). Production
  source, citations, prompts and QA stay in the repo.
- Checks: render, full decode, duration/codecs, transitions/frames, English narration, caption sync, clipping and
  missing assets.
- Attach the finished MP4 **and thumbnail**.
- Report limitations honestly. Never claim a watched or listened check that was not performed. In this cloud session,
  "listening" is an ASR proxy and must be described as such.
- This app channel is educational and separate from any unrelated Discover short-film request.
