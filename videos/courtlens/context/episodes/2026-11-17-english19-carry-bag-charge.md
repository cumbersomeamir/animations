## Final English script (as spoken) [VERIFIED]
"Eighteen rupees for their bag? The billing counter just added it to your bill. The National Consumer Commission said:
shops can sell bags, but buying one must be your choice. Printed with the shop's name or logo? Then they can't charge
for it. They can't stop you bringing your own empty bag. And notices at the entrance must tell you before you shop.
Logo bag? Ask for it free. Plain bag? Say no, or bring your own. Follow Courtlens for everyday Indian law explained."

## Citations [VERIFIED; full text in production notes/LEGAL-SOURCES.md]
- **NCDRC, Bata India Ltd v. Dinesh Parshad Raturi, RP 1715/2019 with RP 774/2020, 27 Nov 2024, paras 23.1–23.5:**
  - no duty to give bags free
  - buying one is optional
  - no charge for a bag with the seller's name or logo
  - empty bags may be brought in
  - notices, including at the entrance, before purchase
- **NCDRC, Big Bazaar (Future Retail Ltd.) v. Ashok Kumar, RP 975/2020, 22 Dec 2020:** prior notice before choosing
  the shop and the goods.
- **Consistent later orders (notes only):**
  - Uttarakhand State Commission, 30 Oct 2026 (informed purchase; no law mandates free bags)
  - South Delhi District Commission, 26 May 2026
- No Supreme Court ruling on carry-bag charges was found (17 Nov 2026 search).
