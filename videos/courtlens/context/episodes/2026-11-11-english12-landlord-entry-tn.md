## Feedback applied [USER DIRECTIVE, 11 Nov 2026]
- Feedback: dense text, too many facts, weak motion and weak thumbnail.
  - Now one door/key/tenant visual leads, then one condition at a time; labels of 1–3 words.
  - Motion carries meaning: key turn, door swing, light spill, shadow, notice sliding in, the 24-hour ring filling,
    the 7–8 arc lighting, the key withdrawing, the gavel.
  - A separate thumbnail with 4 big words.
- Hook: a key opening a door, with "THEIR HOUSE. YOUR HOME?" as both the spoken hook and the giant caption.
- Ownership vs possession/privacy conflict, then agreed access (with notice, by day) vs unauthorised entry (no
  notice, at night) in contrasting halves.
- Payoff: the verified Tamil Nadu rule and a first step ("ask them to come back with one"). The forum is the Rent
  Court.
- Jurisdiction: Tamil Nadu only, not universalised. There is one concise state qualifier and no boilerplate
  disclaimer.
- Captions are inside x 100..900 (centre 500), within y 230..1500.

## Final English script (as spoken) [VERIFIED]
"Their house. Your home? Can your landlord just let themselves in? In Tamil Nadu, the tenancy law draws the line.
They may enter for repairs, an inspection, or a reason in your agreement, with written notice at least twenty-four
hours ahead, saying why, which day, and a time between seven a.m. and eight p.m. No notice? Ask them to come back with
one. If it keeps happening, the Rent Court can hear it. Follow Courtlens for everyday Indian law explained."

## Citations [VERIFIED; full text in production notes/LEGAL-SOURCES.md]
- TN Act 42 of 2017:
  - s.17(1)(a)–(c) and (2): entry with notice, as enacted
  - TN Act 19 of 2022 changes s.17(1) to allow notice "in writing or through electronic mode"; its commencement is
    unconfirmed
  - s.34 (2018 numbering): Rent Court jurisdiction
