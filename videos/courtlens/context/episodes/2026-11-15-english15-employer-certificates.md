## Brief applied [USER DIRECTIVE, 15 Nov 2026]
- **First frame:** an original degree visibly locked behind an office glass drawer, with "YOUR DEGREE. THEIR LOCK?" at
  t = 0. Hook audio starts at 0.05 s. The first frame doubles as the thumbnail.
- **Custody vs consent vs coercion:**
  - handing over at joining = custody with consent (entrustment)
  - keeping originals after you leave, to force payment = "like a ransom" (unlawful in the case)
  - money owed = sue
- **One clear case with accurate scope:** *A. John Paul v. State*, Madras HC, Crl.OP 9920/2012, 10 Jul 2012
  (teachers v. their private school). It is named on screen with year and parties. There is no blanket nationwide
  claim.
- **Practical evidence and request route:** a signed receipt, a written request for return, and every reply kept.
- **Retained feedback:** one central visual (the locked drawer), conditions revealed one at a time, large brief
  labels.

## Final English script (as spoken) [VERIFIED]
"Your degree. Their lock? You resign. They say: pay up, or it stays. Handing originals over when you join? That's
custody, with your consent. But in twenty twelve, the Madras High Court said an employer can't hold them like a ransom.
Money owed? The employer can sue. The court told police to register a breach of trust case. So get a signed receipt,
ask for them back in writing, and keep every reply. Follow Courtlens for everyday Indian law explained."

## Citations [VERIFIED; full text in production notes/LEGAL-SOURCES.md]
- *A. John Paul v. State* (Madras HC, 10.07.2012), paras 10, 17, 23, 24: "cannot be retained like a ransom";
  "only entrusted"; "No other person including the employer has got right to retain the certificates after the
  candidate has left his service"; police directed to register a s.406 IPC case.
- Supporting, not narrated: *Dr. Devender Pal Singh Tomar v. UoI* (Delhi HC, W.P.(C) 7230/2017, 16.03.2018): degrees
  cannot be retained "by way of ransom" absent a bond clause.
- IPC ss.405–406 are now BNS s.316 (from 1 Jul 2024). No section number is used in the video.
