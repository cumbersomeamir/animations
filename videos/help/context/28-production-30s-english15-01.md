  Development Economics 139: 249–265 — published abstract (EconPapers): "Staggered implementation across different states of a 2001 Indian Supreme
  Court Directive mandating the introduction of free school lunches in public primary schools generates plausibly exogenous variation in program
  exposure… We find that prolonged exposure to midday meals has a robust positive effect on learning achievement." Working-paper text (IZA DP 10086,
  2016, local SHA-256 68a8f7a1…4b89f): "it provided lunch to 120 million children in government primary schools on every school day" (2006) ·
  rationale "they encourage enrollment and attendance… alleviation of short-term hunger facilitates concentration" · caveat "Presumably
  complementary teaching inputs are also required" · "children generally enjoy the opportunity to sit with their peers and eat their midday meals"
  (anecdotal, citing Drèze and Goyal 2003).
- **S2** Afridi, F. (2011) "The Impact of School Meals on School Participation: Evidence from Rural India", Journal of Development Studies 47(11):
  1636–1656 — abstract (EconPapers): transition "from monthly distribution of free food grains to the daily provision of free cooked meals… had a
  significant impact on improving the daily participation rates of children in lower grades. The average monthly attendance rate of girls in grade
  one was more than 12 percentage points higher while there was a positive but insignificant effect on grade one boys' attendance rate. The impact on
  enrolment levels was insignificant."
- **S3** Afridi, F., Barooah, B. & Somanathan, R. (2016) "The Mixture as Before? Student Responses to the Changing Content of School Meals in India",
  IZA DP 9924 (local SHA-256 b9ab7a3f…c852): Delhi municipal schools "switched from packaged food to on-site cooked meals in 2003… cooked meals
  resulted in a 3-4 percentage point rise in average monthly attendance with the largest effects observed for early grades."
