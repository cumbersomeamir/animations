shop; explain the up-front cash barrier, not "poor people make bad choices". Format "Hands That Reveal the Hidden Cost" (inspiration

## Sources (accessed 2026-11-02)
- **P1** University of Bristol Personal Finance Research Centre (Davies, Evans et al.), *The poverty premium in 2022: Progress and problems*
  (published April 2023), https://bristol.ac.uk/media-library/sites/geography/pfrc/documents/The%20poverty%20premium%20in%202022%20-%20Progress%20and%20problems.pdf
  (SHA-256 `c373902c…a850276`): "The poverty premium is the term used to describe how those in or near poverty – living on low incomes – pay more for
  essential goods and services than those who are not." (cites Caplovitz 1963, *The poor pay more*).
- **P2** Orhun, A. Y. & Palazzolo, M. (2019), "Frugality Is Hard to Afford", *Journal of Marketing Research* 56(1), doi:10.1177/0022243718821660
  (original study; US household purchase data). Read via the American Marketing Association summary (2020-03-20),
  https://www.ama.org/2020/03/20/frugality-is-hard-to-afford/ : low-income households "buy smaller package sizes (at a higher unit price)";
  "they struggle to buy in bulk and on sale"; "When low-income consumers have more liquidity … they do take advantage of bulk discounts and
  sales"; "it's not simply a lack of knowledge or awareness of sales and bulk discounts that drives the problem."
