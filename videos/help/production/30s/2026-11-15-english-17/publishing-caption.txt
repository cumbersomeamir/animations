One step can stop a wheelchair. Here's the fix.
So how gentle must the ramp be?

India's accessibility standard: at most 1 in 12 for short ramps.
Every centimetre up needs twelve centimetres along.
A fifteen-centimetre step needs about 1.8 metres of ramp.
With handrails, a non-slip surface and a level landing.

Giving a wheelchair? Check the doorstep: funding a proper ramp may be the real gift.

Sources:
- Bureau of Indian Standards, IS 4963:1987 "Recommendations for Buildings and Facilities for the Physically Handicapped", clause 5.1 (a ramp "should not have a slope greater than 1 in 20 or maximum of 1 in 12 for short distance up to 9 000 mm"; handrails 900 mm high on at least one side and preferably both; non-slip surface; level platform at the top where a door opens onto it). Public copy (Public.Resource.Org): https://archive.org/details/gov.in.is.4963.1987
- Barrier Free Consultancy Cell, College of Engineering Trivandrum, "Ready Reference for Barrier-Free Built Environments" (October 2021): ramp table with 1:12 for level differences of 150-750 mm, handrails on both sides: https://www.cet.ac.in/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/ready-reference-booklet.pdf
