One heavy coat, or several thin layers: which is warmer?
And one common way to keep warm can kill.

Health advisories pick layers: they keep body heat trapped inside.
Stay dry: wet clothes pull heat from the body.
Cover the head, hands and feet.
And never burn coal or wood in a closed room.
It makes carbon monoxide, a gas you can't see or smell.

So for winter donations, give layers, caps and socks.

Sources:
- Directorate of Health Services, Government of NCT of Delhi (National Programme for Climate Change and Human Health), Public Health Advisory "Protect yourself from cold wave": "Wear multiple layers of loose fitting, lightweight, windproof warm woollen clothing rather than one layer of heavy clothing"; keep dry; "Use hats and mufflers to prevent heat loss"; "Do not burn candles, woods, etc. in closed, unventilated rooms and prevent carbon monoxide (CO) poisoning": https://dgehs.delhi.gov.in/sites/default/files/DGHS/circulars-orders/phii.pdf
- India Meteorological Department, "Dos and Don'ts for Cold Wave": multiple layers of clothing; "Keep dry. If wet, change clothes quickly to prevent loss of body heat": https://mausam.imd.gov.in/mumbai/mcdata/eng_cwave.pdf
- U.S. National Library of Medicine, MedlinePlus, "How to prevent frostbite and hypothermia" (reviewed 15 Jul 2026; several layers keep body heat trapped inside): https://medlineplus.gov/ency/patientinstructions/000866.htm
- U.S. EPA, "Protect your family and yourself from carbon monoxide poisoning" ("You can't see or smell carbon monoxide, but at high levels it can kill a person in minutes"; CO comes from burning wood or charcoal): https://www.epa.gov/indoor-air-quality-iaq/protect-your-family-and-yourself-carbon-monoxide-poisoning
