This food bank looks like a shop, on purpose.
No packed bags: people pick their own food.

Won't people just take too much?
Pantries found the opposite: people often take too little.
They skip what they can't eat, so less is thrown away.
In a Connecticut trial, choice plus coaching beat food bags on food security.

Choice isn't a luxury: food that fits gets eaten, and people feel respected.

To donate essentials in Lucknow, download Help.

(Illustrative diagram. Help does not run food pantries.)

Sources:
- University of Missouri, Interdisciplinary Center for Food Security, "Power Up Your Pantry: Supporting Client Choice" (client choice is like shopping at a grocery store instead of pre-packaged boxes; the assumption that clients take too much was largely unfounded, and clients often do not take enough; less food waste; clients feel more respected): https://foodsecurity.missouri.edu/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/PUYP-Guide-Sheet_Client-Choice-FINAL.pdf
- Martin KS et al., "A novel food pantry program: food security, self-sufficiency, and diet-quality outcomes," American Journal of Preventive Medicine, 2013 (randomised trial of Freshplace in Hartford, Connecticut: a client-choice pantry with monthly coaching and referrals, compared with traditional pantries giving bags of food): https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24139769/
- NORC at the University of Chicago, evaluation of Feeding America's food choice work (clients at full-choice pantries report more satisfaction and less food waste): https://www.norc.org/research/projects/feeding-americas-child-family-choice-program.html
