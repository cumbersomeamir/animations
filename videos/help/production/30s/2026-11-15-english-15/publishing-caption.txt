An empty seat, an empty lunchbox: they're connected.

Fictional example: on no-lunch days, Asha stays home.
What brings her back, ready to learn?

India's midday meal: a free hot lunch in government schools.
Two studies found cooked school meals raised attendance, most for the youngest.
And years of these meals are linked to better reading and maths.

Not a lunchbox for one child, but a daily meal for the whole class.

Sources:
- Chakraborty, T. & Jayaraman, R. (2019), "School feeding and learning achievement: Evidence from India's midday meal program," Journal of Development Economics 139: 249–265 (prolonged exposure to midday meals had a robust positive effect on children's math and reading test scores): https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:deveco:v:139:y:2019:i:c:p:249-265 (open working-paper version: https://docs.iza.org/dp10086.pdf)
- Afridi, F. (2011), "The Impact of School Meals on School Participation: Evidence from Rural India," Journal of Development Studies 47(11): 1636–1656 (moving from monthly free grain to daily cooked meals raised attendance in the lower grades, especially first-grade girls): https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:taf:jdevst:v:47:y:2011:i:11:p:1636-1656
- Afridi, F., Barooah, B. & Somanathan, R. (2016), "The Mixture as Before? Student Responses to the Changing Content of School Meals in India," IZA Discussion Paper 9924 (in Delhi municipal schools, cooked meals instead of packaged food raised attendance, most in the early grades): https://docs.iza.org/dp9924.pdf
