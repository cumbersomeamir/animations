## Disclosed simplifications
- **Feather pile size is illustrative.** The sack is ≈ 0.056 m³ (≈ 430× the cube's volume, implied ≈ 18 kg/m³). No
  credible single bulk-density value for "feathers" was found; real values vary with feather type and compression (S4),
  so only "far more space" is claimed.
- **No buoyancy discussion (per the brief).** A real equal-arm balance in air would read the 1 kg of feathers very
  slightly low, because air buoyancy acts on the feathers' larger material volume (a fraction of a percent; not shown).
  The question and answer are stated in terms of mass, which is exactly equal by definition.
- Round-2 masses (≈ 50 kg of solid steel vs ≈ 0.1 kg of feathers in an 18.6 cm box) follow from the same illustrative
  pile density; on screen only the direction ("far more mass") is shown.
