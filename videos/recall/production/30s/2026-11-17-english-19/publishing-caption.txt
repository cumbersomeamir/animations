Hotter than boiling water.

A pressure cooker keeps water liquid above 100 degrees. Water boils when its vapor pushes as hard as the air above it. At sea level, that's 100 degrees. Lock the lid, and steam can't escape, so the pressure rises. So the water must reach about 120 degrees to boil. Hotter water cooks food faster. On a mountain it's the reverse: water boils cooler, so food cooks slower.
