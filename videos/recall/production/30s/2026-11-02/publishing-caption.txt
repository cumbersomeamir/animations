Can you beat this fifth-grade science question?

A steel ball sinks. A steel ship floats. Same material, so what changes?

Answer: the ship's hollow shape displaces much more water for its weight. Water pushes up with a force equal to the weight of the water displaced (Archimedes' principle). When that push balances the ship's weight, it floats. Fill the hull with water and it sinks. The steel never got lighter; the shape changed the balance.
