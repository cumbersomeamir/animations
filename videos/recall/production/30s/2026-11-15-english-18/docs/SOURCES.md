Read on 2026-11-15 as page text in headless Chromium (OpenStax, U. Illinois Physics Van, HyperPhysics, Royal Institution) and as a
PDF (NIST). Britannica returned a bot-check page and two OpenStax-derived course sites (MSU Open Books, UCF Pressbooks) returned

## Topic check
The suggested topic (vacuum flask: vacuum gap, shiny walls, lid) was never covered. E15 ended with "An insulated cup stays dry"
(an example inside a condensation episode) but did not explain how vacuum walls or silvering work; no episode covers conduction,
convection and radiation in a flask. The topic was kept.

## Verbatim quotes
- **S1:** "The space between the Earth and the Sun is largely empty, without any possibility of heat transfer by convection or conduction." "…no medium is required for electromagnetic waves to propagate." "The radiation you feel is mostly infrared…" "A white object reflects all radiation, like a mirror." "…a white (or silver) object is a poor absorber and a poor radiator."
- **S2:** "The thermos just slows down the flow of heat between its contents and the outside world. So whatever temperature the contents have, they tend to keep." "In a thermos bottle the air is pumped out between two vessels. That reduces convection. In addition, the surfaces are silvered. That reduces radiation heat transfer."
- **S3:** "Clearly, the flask is able to effectively block heat transfer from the outside either through the walls or through the open top."
- **S4:** "This vessel consists of glass flasks fitted one inside the other and sealed at the neck with a partial vacuum between them." "This initial design was later improved by narrowing the neck even further and putting a silvered coating on the outside to minimise heat loss."
- **S5:** "…it was found that the residual gas between the walls was responsible for one-fourth the heat transferred across the vacuum space of the flask filled with liquid air."

## Measured facts, estimates and illustrative assumptions
| item | status |
|---|---|
| Heat moves by conduction (solids), convection (moving air/fluid) and radiation (infrared) | textbook (S1, §14.4–14.7) |
| Two walls with a (partial) vacuum between them; almost no air → conduction and convection across the gap are greatly reduced | S2, S4; S1 (empty space allows neither) |
| Infrared radiation crosses a vacuum (like sunlight through space) | S1 |
| Shiny (silvered) walls reduce radiative transfer | S2, S4; S1 (silver surfaces are poor absorbers/emitters, mirror-like) |
| The top (neck and lid) is the remaining weak spot | S3 (heat through the open top), S4 (neck narrowed to minimise heat loss). The share of heat lost through the top is **not quantified** |
| Widened gap, wall thickness, arrow counts and speeds, the zig-zag "bounce" path | **illustrative** |
