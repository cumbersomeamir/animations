## Sources (quotes, claim map and facts-vs-assumptions in the output folder's `docs/SOURCES.md`)
- **Stenfelt 2011 (Adv Otorhinolaryngol 71):** "Bone conduction (BC) is the way sound energy is transmitted by the skull bones to
  the cochlea causing a sound perception."
- **Reinfeldt et al. 2010 (JASA 128):** own voice has a BC part and an AC part; "the BC part of one's own voice dominated at
  frequencies between 1 and 2 kHz for most of the phonemes."
- **Orepic et al. 2023 (R Soc Open Sci 10):** a recorded voice "is perceived as strange"; "One of the main reasons is the lack
  of bone conduction"; bone conduction is for "our own voice, and our own voice only"; it "transforms the sound of our voice".
- **Stenfelt & Reinfeldt 2007; Denk et al. 2023 (Int J Audiol):** occluding the ear canal gives "a level increase of
  bone-conducted low-frequency components" of your own voice.
- PubMed (HTTP 403) and the ASHA journal page (bot check) were not retried or circumvented and are not cited.
- **Facts vs assumptions:** the two paths, the missing bone part in recordings and the occlusion effect are stated by the
  sources; the popular "bone makes you sound deeper" claim is **not** used (S3 calls the low-pass effect "assumed"; S2 measured
  the bone part dominating at 1–2 kHz); fingers in the ears as occlusion is an everyday inference; anatomy, pulse paths and the
  hum sound are illustrative.
