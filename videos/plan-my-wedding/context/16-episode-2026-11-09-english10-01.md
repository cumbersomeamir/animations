## Sources (primary; publishing notes only)
- **UK Health and Safety Executive**, *Crowd management: controlling crowds inside venues*: keeping routes clear "will help avoid congestion, which if left unchecked, could lead to 'pinch-points' and overcrowding." This supports avoiding a single merge point. https://www.hse.gov.uk/event-safety/crowd-management-control-inside.htm
- **UK HSE**, *Crowd management controls*: "Take reasonable steps to protect people queuing on traffic routes and stairs." This supports planning where queues start and exit. https://www.hse.gov.uk/event-safety/crowd-management-controls.htm
- **US DOJ, 2010 ADA Standards for Accessible Design** (reference for what an accessible route means). https://www.ada.gov/law-and-regs/design-standards/2010-stds/
  - **§402.2:** accessible routes consist of components such as walking surfaces, ramps, curb ramps, elevators and platform lifts.
  - **§303.4:** "Changes in level greater than 1/2 inch (13 mm) high shall be ramped…". This backs "step-free" and the ramp.
  - **§904.5.1:** "Self-service shelves and dispensing devices for tableware, dishware, condiments, food and beverages shall comply with 308" (reach ranges). This backs "food within reach".
- **Plan My Wedding Google Play listing** (assets/listing.json): "Build a personalised checklist…". This supports the CTA. https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.tensorblue.planmywedding
