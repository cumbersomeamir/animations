The ad that admits a weakness.

Volkswagen's 1959 "Think small." campaign, created by the Doyle Dane Bernbach agency, didn't pretend the Beetle was big. It made smallness the point: white space, plain words and a modest photo, when most cars on sale in the U.S. were bigger and flashier.

The lesson isn't to copy that layout. Find a real trade-off your product makes, then show why the right customer might prefer it. A limitation can become a reason to choose.

The 1959 layout shown is an analytical reconstruction, not the original ad. The compact-tool example is fictional.

Sources:
- German Historical Institute, Transatlantic Perspectives: "Doyle Dane Bernbach" (DDB won the Volkswagen account in 1959; "Think Small" focused on the Beetle's small form, in contrast to most cars sold in the U.S. at the time): https://www.transatlanticperspectives.org/entry.php?rec=35
- The Henry Ford, "Advertisement for Volkswagen of America, 'Think Small,' 1962" (a later printing held in its collection; describes the ads as "simple, honest, and successful"): https://www.thehenryford.org/collections/explore/artifact/368680
- Advertising Age, "The Advertising Century" (1999), ranked the Volkswagen "Think Small" campaign (DDB, 1959) first among the century's campaigns (as cited by the German Historical Institute above).
