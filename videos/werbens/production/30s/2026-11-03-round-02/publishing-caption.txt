Why do brands repeat one shape everywhere?

Picture a fictional bakery that puts the same arch on its logo, its bag and its sign. Cover the name, and the arch can still point you to the bakery. Researchers call this a distinctive brand asset, but it only works once enough buyers have learned the link. Swap shapes on every item and there's nothing consistent to learn.

Takeaway: pick one asset, use it the same way everywhere, and test whether people link it to you with the name hidden. Research comparing asset types found logos, logotypes and characters easier to own than colour, which competitors often share.

The bakery and its arch are fictional.
