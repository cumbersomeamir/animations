## 7. How the rules were applied (creative decisions)
- **Frame 0 = thumbnail (per the brief: strong first frame).** A low, close view of the top two shelves of a tall cooler: generic
  teal bottles with mint caps and first-name labels (TOM, AMY, BEN, LUCY, KAI / ADAM, IRIS, OMAR, MIA, JADE), a search ring and
  glow on BEN; the headline "FIND YOUR / NAME?" sits in the dark space above the cooler (Space Grotesk 700; second line in the
  hero gradient). No intro, no fade. The ring darts between names through the hook.
- **Original, clearly illustrative packs:** no Coca-Cola logo, red, contour bottle or "You" typeface; the "logo" side of each
  label is a plain circle-and-dot mark; names are common first names; no sales or volume figures.
- **One concrete mechanism, shown as purchase behaviour:** labels spin from the plain logo to names in a wave ("swapped the logo
  for popular first names"); five more coolers rise so the wall holds exactly 6 × 25 = 150 bottles on "150 names"; a search
  light zigzags shelf by shelf and dims the names it has checked (THE SEARCH), ending on ALEX next to SAM; a stream of name cards
  rushes past and SAM's card stops and pops (YOUR NAME POPS) and flies into the cooler, where the SAM bottle slides out (found);
  the friend's bottle (MIA) glows, slides out and gets a ribbon (A GIFT); in the payoff SAM holds his bottle while the gift arcs
  to MIA (FIND + GIVE).
- **Choreography varied, identity kept:** this episode's camera grammar is the eye's search — darting light, push to the labels,
  a diagonal pull-back along the wall of coolers (solved by projection so all six fit below the label), a shelf-by-shelf scan, a
  static name stream in front of the lens, a close on the friend's bottle and a two-shot hand-over. Same studio, palette,
  captions, logo tile and lockup.
- **Labels over a busy set:** the cooler fills most shots, so the big labels get a subtle dark drop-shadow halo
  (`filter: drop-shadow`) for contrast — colours, font and size unchanged.
- **Caption band (context/27 §6):** pill y 293–447, ink y 309–429; one label at a time at y ≈ 630.
- **Reusable lessons:**
  1. Text-on-props episodes (names on labels): build one canvas atlas and give each label a texture clone with its own
     repeat/offset — 150 legible labels at little cost.
  2. A cylinder label with `thetaStart = π` puts u = 0.5 at the front, so the name sits front-centre and the reverse side can
     carry a second design (logo); spinning the label by π swaps them.
  3. When a frame must show "all N" (here 6 coolers = 150), solve the camera numerically so every object fits inside the safe
     area below the label.
  4. A shot can leave a subject out entirely (the shopper in the search) rather than show a stray cropped piece of it.
