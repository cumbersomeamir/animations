## 4. Facts used (verified 2026-11-27 from primary sources) — tagged
1. **The Coca-Cola Company, "How a Groundbreaking Campaign Got Its Start 'Down Under'"** (page dated 2014-09-25;
   https://www.coca-colacompany.com/about-us/history/how-a-campaign-got-its-start-down-under): the campaign swapped "Coke
   branding on bottles and cans with the 150 most popular monikers in Australia"; "We started with 150."; "people were buying
   Cokes to show people they cared for that they missed them"; "we purposefully made the invitation more about giving a Coke to
   someone else rather than keeping it to yourself."
2. **The Coca-Cola Company, "Coca-Cola Celebrates a Summer of Sharing"** (press release, 2014-06-10, investors.coca-colacompany.com
   PDF): US 20-ounce bottles swap logos "for 250 of the nation's most popular names among teens and Millennials"; the company
   is "inviting people to find their name and the names of family, friends, colleagues, or even the names of people they want
   to know better"; website visitors "can find out which names are available in-stores"; "For those unable to find specific
   names on store shelves", a tour let people customise a mini can "for themselves and a second can for someone special".
3. **The Coca-Cola Company, "'Share a Coke' Returns with 1,000 Reasons to Enjoy a Coke This Summer"** (press release,
   2015-04-14): 1,000 names, "quadrupling the number of names featured in 2014"; "For those who aren't able to find their
   names – or enough of a particular name – on store shelves" (online customisation).
4. **The Coca-Cola Company, "Iconic 'Share a Coke' is Back for a New Generation"** (2025-03-26): "In 2011, Coca‑Cola
   Australia launched what became an iconic marketing campaign … swapping out the logo with popular first names";
   "consumers will begin finding Coca‑Cola cans or bottles with names on store shelves"; rollout to 120+ countries.
   Launch-year note: sources differ (2011 in 1 and 4; "summer of 2011-12" in 3; 2012 in 2) — the narration gives no year.
5. **Shapiro, K. L., Caldwell, J., & Sorensen, R. E. (1997).** Personal names and the attentional blink: A visual "cocktail
   party" effect. *Journal of Experimental Psychology: Human Perception and Performance*, 23(2), 504–514.
   https://doi.org/10.1037/0096-1523.23.2.504 — abstract (OpenAlex): "individuals do not experience an AB for their own names
   but do for either other names or nouns."
