When attitudes are based on advertising, however, attitude-behavior consistency is significantly reduced." |
| L4 | "Field tests found samples brought in new buyers, with effects up to a year later." | **Measured finding** (two field experiments; brands unnamed) | Bawa, K., & Shoemaker, R. (2004). Make the sample prove your main claim." | **Practical advice** (not a measured conversion effect) | Derived from the two findings above: trial-based opinions are more predictive, and effects depend on the brand. Advice is to sample when the experience itself demonstrates the claim. |

## 7. How the rules were applied (creative decisions)
- **Frame 0 = thumbnail.** A composed, lit booth:
  - an untouched ad poster on an easel (product hero + "!" claim bubble, no text);
  - a tasting tray of sample cups, one lifting away in a mint glow;
  - the headline "TRY BEATS / TELL?" (3 words, Space Grotesk 700, hero gradient), centred at x 500 inside x 152–848.

  No intro, fade or empty setup. The headline fades by 0.42 s as the first caption group appears; the two never show together.
  The thumbnail PNG is the same frame plus the logo/wordmark row.
- **One real question:** "why can a sample sell what a perfect ad can't?"
  - The build-up gives the mechanism in one line (believe vs check).
  - Proof 1 makes it credible (trial-based opinions predict buying better).
  - Proof 2 gives the documented measured outcome (new buyers, effects up to 12 months) and its qualification (varies widely
    between brands).
  - The payoff gives two concrete actions.
- **One visual idea at a time, revealed in sequence:**
  1. Samples are taken one by one while the ad stays untouched (sheen on "perfect ad").
  2. The claim bubble swells: "BELIEVE?".
  3. A check over the tray: "CHECK".
  4. Lateral move to the study set: TRIED / AD bases. LIKE columns rise equal. TRIED's BUY rises to match; AD's BUY stops short.
  5. Lateral move to the field-test set: 5 new-buyer tokens hop out of a sample. The glow travels across 12 month tiles, fading:
     "12 MONTHS".
  6. Row B stays faint: "VARIES".
  7. Back to the booth: the tray refills, "LET THEM TRY". The ad's claim bubble flies onto the samples and becomes a check:
     "PROVE THE CLAIM".
  8. The booth parts and the logo tile pops up.
- **Labels:** BELIEVE? / CHECK / 12 MONTHS / VARIES / LET THEM TRY / PROVE THE CLAIM — large, brief, one at a time, y ≥ 562.
  Small in-world chips (TRIED, AD, LIKE, BUY, A, B). No source chips, no paragraph cards, no decorative lines.
- **Reusable lessons:**
  1. A portrait product "booth" reads best stacked: ad behind and above, tray in front. Keep its top below the caption/headline
     band.
  2. Stagger the back row of small props by half a pitch.
  3. A frame-0 headline is caption-equivalent. It needs its own QA bounds and must never coexist with a caption or label.
  4. Reword homophones that change meaning ("won" vs "one").
  5. Stress weak function words after verbs ("sell what").
