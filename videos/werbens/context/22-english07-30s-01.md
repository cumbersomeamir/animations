## 5. Facts used (verified 2026-11-06)
| Claim (line) | Source |
|---|---|
| L5 "In a 2006 study, one extreme price in a catalog raised what people would pay for other items." | Krishna, A., Wagner, M., Yoon, C., & Adaval, R. (2006). Effects of Extreme-Priced Products on Consumer Reservation Prices. *Journal of Consumer Psychology*, 16(2), 176–190. https://doi.org/10.1207/s15327663jcp1602_8 (Crossref metadata; abstract via OpenAlex): "an extremely high-priced product featured among more moderately priced products within a catalog can increase the reservation price for a moderately priced target product as well as the category as a whole … the presence of an extreme cue leads to greater changes in target reservation price when the extreme-priced referent and target are more related and are contiguously presented." ("would pay" = reservation price.) |
| L4 "That's anchoring: the high price sets the reference." | Tversky, A., & Kahneman, D. (1974). Judgment under Uncertainty: Heuristics and Biases. *Science*, 185(4157), 1124–1131. https://doi.org/10.1126/science.185.4157.1124 — abstract: "adjustment from an anchor, which is usually employed in numerical prediction when a relevant value is available … lead[s] to systematic and predictable errors"; Krishna et al. call the extreme item the "extreme-priced referent". |
| L2–L3 "Beside 150-rupee snacks, it can look pricey. No study values or conversion numbers are shown. |
| L6 "So list a real premium item next to it" | Krishna et al. (2006): larger effect when the referent is related and presented contiguously → a real, related premium item placed beside the everyday one. "Real" = an item actually offered (honesty rule below). |
| L6 "and never fake a 'was' price." | US FTC Guides Against Deceptive Pricing, 16 CFR §233.1 "Former price comparisons" (eCFR, read 2026-11-06): a former price is legitimate only if it is "the actual, bona fide price at which the article was offered to the public on a regular basis for a reasonably substantial period of time"; if "fictitious—for example, where an artificial, inflated price was established for the purpose of enabling the subsequent offer of a large reduction—the 'bargain' being advertised is a false one." https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-233/section-233.1 · ASCI Code, Chapter I "Truthful & honest representation" (re-read 2026-11-05): "Advertisements must be truthful." https://www.ascionline.in/the-asci-code/ |
