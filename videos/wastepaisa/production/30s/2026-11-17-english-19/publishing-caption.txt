Old jeans, new bag.

But which pairs are worth cutting? A Mumbai start-up sold almost 3,000 products from old jeans in its first year. Reject thin or stretchy pairs, sort by shade, then wash and cut squares.

Ten pairs, about ₹120 of denim, make three bags and thirty patches. After labour and supplies, about ₹300 is left; the patches pay for the denim. So sort first, then cut. (Denim at a reported ₹20/kg; every other figure is an assumption for illustration, not a market price or a promise.)

Dwij, Mumbai: old jeans segregated by quality, cleaned, cut and tailored; denim bought at about ₹20/kg; almost 3,000 products sold in its first year (The Better India, 2019)
