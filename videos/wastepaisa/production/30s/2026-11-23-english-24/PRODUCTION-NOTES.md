## Research (accessed 23 Nov 2026). Facts, listings and assumptions are kept apart
- **R1, Hartmann India, "About Us"** (manufacturer; formerly Mohan Fibres, operating since 1990, acquired by Denmark's Hartmann in 2020):
  https://hartmann-packaging.com/in/about-us/
  - "We transform recycled paper into protective, functional packaging". Hartmann serves "primarily egg producers and packers supplying
    retailers" and calls itself "the world's leading manufacturer of moulded fibre egg packaging".
  - This is the actual industrial use behind L1 and L2. A search-result snippet of its India home page says "crafted from recycled paper,
    such as newspapers", but that phrase was not on the pages fetched, so the newspaper link rests on R2 and R3.
- **R2, Government of Uttar Pradesh, MSME 1-Connect, "Egg Tray" business category** (official portal):
  https://msme1connect.up.gov.in/Home/BusinessDetails?Id=37
  - Egg trays are made "using recycled paper pulp". The process is "collection of waste paper, pulping to form slurry, molding into tray
    shapes using molds, drying, quality inspection, and packaging".
  - The buyers are "poultry farms, egg wholesalers, retail stores, and transport companies". The machinery is "pulping machines, molding
    machines, drying ovens, and packing equipment".
  - Source for L2's buyer, L3–L5's steps, and the not-a-home-business framing.
- **R3, Debnath, Sarder, Pal and Hubbe, "Molded Pulp Products for Sustainable Packaging…", BioResources 17(2), 2022** (peer-reviewed review):
  https://bioresources.cnr.ncsu.edu/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/BioRes_17_2_3810_Review_-Debnath_SPH_Molded_Pulp_Sustainable_Packaging_19959.pdf
  - Uses: "egg trays and the shipping of fruits… increasingly used for the packaging of electronics, wine bottles". It is used "in
    applications requiring cushioning ability". Transfer-moulded products "are typically used as egg trays and packaging for electronic
    equipment".
  - Process: "an aqueous suspension of fibers in a vat. A porous mold is inserted into the vat, and vacuum is applied to draw fibers… toward
    a screen surface… then released from the mold and subjected to oven drying". Operations start "often with a consistency of about 4%"
    (shown as "PULP ≈ 96% WATER").
  - "the dewatering of molded pulp tends to be slow, and the subsequent evaporation of water is energy-intensive". This is why drying
    is a separate, large cost line.
  - Raw material: "recycled pulps are very widely used by the molded pulp industry"; types studied include "old newspaper… recycled
    cardboard… and old corrugated containers". Source, with R2, for L1's "old newspaper".
  - Fibres bonding as they dry is standard papermaking science; it is the plain-language mechanism for L5.
- **R4, waste-paper asking prices (search-result snippets of scrap-rate and B2B listing pages, 23 Nov 2026):**
  - Newspaper scrap about ₹12–18/kg (Scraprates, India average about ₹14.80/kg in May 2026): https://scraprates.in/mundi/newspaper-scrap-price
  - Used duplex scrap listed at ₹18/kg: https://m.indiamart.com/impcat/waste-paper.html
- **R5, tray asking prices and weights (listing snippets, 23 Nov 2026):**
  - 30-egg pulp trays at ₹3.80, ₹4.50 and ₹5 per piece: https://m.indiamart.com/impcat/paper-egg-tray.html
  - ₹4 per piece with a 1,000-piece minimum order (Vijayawada maker): https://www.exportersindia.com/vijayawada/paper-egg-tray.htm
  - Listed tray weights range from 50 to 90 g; one exporter packs 100–140 trays in 10–12 kg.
- **R6, machine listings (snippets, 23 Nov 2026):**
  - A semi-automatic paper egg-tray machine at 1,400 pieces/hour, 20 kW, ₹16,50,000: https://m.indiamart.com/kk-trays

### Illustrative shift (on screen as "ASSUMED"): one 8-hour shift on a semi-automatic line (~1,400 trays/hour nameplate, R6)
| Item | ₹ | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Output: 8,000 good 30-egg trays (≈ 11,200 nameplate, minus downtime and rejects) | — | assumption |
| Waste paper ≈ 600 kg (8,000 × ~70 g + ~7 % loss) × ₹15/kg | 9,000 | R4 range; R5 weights |
| Drying fuel (≈ 1.3–1.6 t of water to evaporate at ~25–30 % solids after forming; biomass-fired dryer) | 5,000 | assumption; R3 says drying is energy-intensive |
| Labour (≈ 6 workers + supervisor share) | 4,000 | assumption |
| Machine + power: ≈ 280 kWh (₹3,000) + line ≈ ₹25 lakh (machine, pulper, dryer, moulds) over ~1,500 shifts plus upkeep (₹3,000) | 6,000 | R6 listing; assumption |
| **Costs per shift** | **24,000** | arithmetic |
| **Revenue:** 8,000 × ₹3.50 (bulk price to farms, below R5's ₹3.80–5 listings) | **28,000** | assumption anchored to R5 |
| **Left before overheads** | **≈ 4,000** | arithmetic; about 14 % of revenue |
- **Revenue vs profit:** ₹28,000 is what buyers pay (revenue); about ₹4,000 is left after paper, drying, labour, machine and power.
- **Scale and setting:** a factory line with a pulper, vacuum moulds and a dryer, worth about ₹25 lakh. It is not a hand-tool home
  business, and the video gives no home instructions.
- **Not modelled:** rent, water, packing film, delivery, GST, loan interest, unsold stock, seasonal drying losses and the time to win
  buyers. No income is promised, and nothing implies lakhs from scrap paper.
