This egg tray was old newspaper.

Egg farms buy these by the thousand. How does wet paper get this strong? Factories beat waste paper into a watery pulp. A mesh mould dips in, and suction pulls fibre onto it. Drying bonds the fibres into a stiff, cushioned tray.

Suppose one shift's 8,000 trays sell for ₹28,000. After paper, drying, labour and machine costs, about ₹4,000 is left. (The shift figures are assumptions for illustration, built from listed asking prices: waste paper about ₹12–18/kg, 30-egg pulp trays about ₹3.80–5 each. They are not a promise of income; a tray line needs a pulper, vacuum moulds and a dryer.)
