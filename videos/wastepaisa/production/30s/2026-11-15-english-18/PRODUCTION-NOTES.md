## Research (accessed 15 Nov 2026). Facts, practice and assumptions are kept apart
- **R1, FREITAG, "About FREITAG"** (company's own site): https://freitag.ch/about-freitag
  - "What began in Zurich in 1993 with messenger bags made from discarded truck tarps".
  - Staff at the Zurich headquarters are "dismantling, washing and cutting truck tarps".
  - Source for L3. The company is named in the notes only, not on screen; the truck on screen is generic.
- **R2, The Better India on Project ReFlex, Gurugram** (22 Aug 2025):
  https://thebetterindia.com/490015/samvit-govindani-shaurya-sharma-project-reflex-upcycling-discarded-pvc-banners-empowering-women
  - The founders collect PVC flex banners, check each one, "cutting away faded or torn sections", and wash them by hand.
  - Tailors turn them into "finished products ready for sale"; tote bags are "a big hit".
  - Products are sold at local events: over 430 banners became over 260 products. No prices are given.
  - Source for L4, and Indian evidence that banner totes sell.
- **R3, banner material:** frontlit flex is a polyester scrim coated with PVC, typically 280–440 gsm. Sources: manufacturer and supplier
  data sheets, e.g. Hexis FRONT banners, and an Indian listing of "PVC Flex banner (Star flex), 280 440 GSM".
- **R4, sewing practice (notes only, not on screen):** vinyl-sewing guides recommend a longer stitch (about 3.0–3.5 mm) because needle
  holes are permanent and close holes act like a perforation; use clips instead of pins, and a Teflon or walking foot. Sources: Quilt
  Social and Marine Vinyl Fabric (search extracts).
- **Context, not used on screen:**
  - NIFT Kolkata (IFFTI Proceedings, Vol. 3, 2024): turning Durga Puja flex banners into a tote; 218 design students surveyed. Only the
    abstract is available, and it gives no demand figures.
  - Swabhimaan (Media4Growth, 2018): "There is a huge market for the second hand flex material; earlier we used to buy them at a minimal
    cost."
- **Prices:** no published price for used banners or banner totes was found. Every rupee figure on screen is an assumption, labelled
  "ASSUMED".

### Assumptions (on screen as "ASSUMED"; minimal labels)
| Item | Value | Why this assumption |
|---|---|---|
| Banner size and yield | one 10×20 ft (~18.6 m²) banner → 20 totes | ~60 % usable after hems, faded and torn parts; ~0.5 m² of flex per tote (two 40×45 cm faces, gusset, seams); rounded down for layout waste |
| Banner cost | ₹200 | used banners are often collected free or bought "at a minimal cost" (R2 and Swabhimaan); a nominal price is used |
| Labour (wash, cut, stitch) | ₹1,000 (₹50 per tote) | piece-rate assumption |
| Handles and thread | ₹500 (₹25 per tote) | webbing and thread assumption |
| **Costs** | **₹1,700** | arithmetic |
| Unsold stock | 4 of 20 | 20 % unsold assumption |
| Selling price | ₹150 per tote, 16 sold | assumption; no verified market price |
| Sales | ₹2,400 | arithmetic (16 × ₹150) |
| **Left (margin)** | **≈ ₹700** | arithmetic (₹2,400 − ₹1,700); ≈29 % of sales, ≈₹44 per tote sold |
- **Not modelled** (each would cut the ₹700): selling costs (stall fees, platform commission, delivery), needle and machine wear,
  electricity, and the maker's own time spent finding buyers.
- **The comparison the brief asked for:** the bulk banner costs ₹200. After the work the totes bring ₹2,400, and about ₹700 is left once
  labour, handles and four unsold bags are paid for. The material is the cheap part.
