## Sources (accessed 2026-11-02)
- **A1:** The Aluminum Association, *Aluminum Beverage Can Design Guide: Four Keys to Circular Recycling* (PDF,
  https://www.aluminum.org/sites/default/files/2021-10/AA_ContainerDesignGuide.pdf). Relevant quotes:
  - "Most MRFs also typically use density, size, magnetic and eddy current machinery to separate aluminum containers…"
  - "the bales are opened, shredded and run through a high temperature process to remove paints, lacquers and coatings.
    The aluminum is then sent to a furnace to be melted and purified. It then may have its alloy content adjusted before
    being cast into an ingot for the next generation of can sheet production."
  - "Slabs are rolled to produce can stocks." "Can manufacturers use can stocks to produce new aluminum cans…
    printed with labels, inner coated."
  - "These cans contain two distinct alloys – one for the container body and another for the container lid and
    open-tab."
  - "…printing inks and coatings that must be removed during recycling."
  - Collection can be curbside, at a local recycling centre, or "under a container deposit system".
  - Plastic labels and sleeves are "diluting the value".
- **A2:** The Aluminum Association, *Aluminum Agenda: Recycling — Infinitely Recyclable, Uniquely Sustainable* (2024,
  PDF, https://www.aluminum.org/sites/default/files/2024-05/2024-Agenda_Recycling-Overview.pdf). Relevant points: "aluminum cans
  can be recycled infinitely to make new cans"; cans are a "valuable beverage package in the recycling bin";
  container deposit laws apply in some states.
- **A3:** U.S. Geological Survey, *Mineral Commodity Summaries 2025: Aluminum* (PDF,
  https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2025/mcs2025-aluminum.pdf). The U.S. spot ingot price moved from 89.7 to 138.5 to
  152.6 to 125.9 to 130 (estimated) cents/lb over 2020–2024, so prices change over time.
