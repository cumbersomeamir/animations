## Research (accessed 14 Nov 2026). Measured facts, market rates, estimates and assumptions are kept apart
- **D1, densities (educational reference):** Chemistry LibreTexts, "Sorting Recyclable Plastics by Density" (ChemPRIME; CC BY-NC-SA 4.0),
  Table 1, g/cm³: PET 1.35–1.38; HDPE 0.94–0.96; PVC 1.32–1.42; LDPE 0.91–0.93; PP 0.90–0.92; PS 1.03–1.06. "The sorting is done by
  floatation in many commercial firms."
  https://chem.libretexts.org/Ancillary_Materials/Exemplars_and_Case_Studies/Exemplars/Environmental_and_Green_chemistry/Sorting_Recyclable_Plastics_by_Density
  Water is 1.00, so PE and PP float and PET and PVC sink.
- **D2, industry practice (search extract):** Herbold Meckesheim density-separation tanks (Recycling Today / Recycling Product News):
  "higher density plastics like PET and PVC and/or contaminants sink to the bottom of the tank while lower density plastics like PE and
  PP float on the surface of the water". A Chulalongkorn University thesis gives the same density ranges and notes HDPE and PP need a
  lighter liquid (ethanol-water) to separate, which is why the episode separates them by resin code instead.
  https://www.recyclingtoday.com/news/herbold-meckesheim-separation-recycling-pet-pp-plastic-scrap-density
- **B1, buyer requirements (Association of Plastic Recyclers model bale specifications; PDFs moved, wording from search extracts):**
  - Mixed bulky rigid (PP/HDPE): other rigids (PET, PS, #7) capped at 4 % combined; "PVC (#3) in any form is excluded"; metal not allowed.
  - PP small rigids: PVC excluded; HDPE/LDPE rigid ≤ 10 %; PET and PS ≤ 3 % combined; other metal, wood, glass excluded.
  - Coloured HDPE bottles: non-HDPE rigids ≤ 2 %; metal and PVC not allowed.
  - https://plasticsrecycling.org/images/Markets/APR-BaleSpec-MixedBulkyRigidPlastic.pdf (and PP / HDPE equivalents).
- **B2, resin identification codes:** the stamped numbers 2 = HDPE and 5 = PP (ASTM D7611 resin identification coding; 3 = PVC, 1 = PET).
- **M1, dated rates (marketplace index):** Scrapo, "Plastic Scrap Rate Today per Kg in India (November 2026)", Scrapo Price Index =
  median asking price, recomputed monthly: HDPE ₹70.69/kg; PP ₹63.15/kg; PVC ₹37.70/kg; ABS ₹56.56/kg; PET ₹49.01/kg. "Loose, mixed
  household plastic sold to a kabadiwala fetches much less", because it has to be sorted, cleaned and baled first.
  https://scrapo.com/in/plastic-scrap-rate
- **M2, household rate (undated):** Kabadiwala Online, Delhi NCR: "Plastic Scrap – Hard | ₹15 – ₹30 | Crates, buckets, chairs, household
  hard plastic". https://kabadiwalaonline.in/price-list/
- **P4 and T1 (costs, from contexts 17 and 24):** Delhi unskilled minimum wage ₹710/day; mini-truck ≈₹3,000 per full day.

### Illustrative per-kilo costs (on-screen "COSTS ARE ILLUSTRATIVE")
| Item | ≈ ₹/kg | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Sorting + cleaning | 6 | one worker-day (₹710, P4) assumed to sort and clean ≈120 kg |
| Transport | 6 | one ≈₹3,000 truck day (T1) for an assumed 500 kg lot |

The episode compares a sorted-HDPE asking index (M1, ≈₹70/kg) with what a mixed hard-plastic pile fetches from a kabadiwala (M2, ₹15–30/kg)
and says the gap has to pay for sorting, cleaning and transport. It states no profit, buying price or yield. Asking prices are not
transaction prices, and the two figures come from different points in the chain (household seller vs business seller); both caveats
are in the on-screen source lines ("ASKING INDEX", "UNDATED").
