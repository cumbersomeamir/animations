## Brief
Teach a practical value check for old cable: inside may be copper or aluminium (or thin data-cable conductors); metal type,
insulation, grade and weight shape a buyer's assessment. Keep types separate, ask how they're priced, never burn insulation.
End on a short WastePaisa CTA. No rupee rates, earnings promises, dismantling or burning demonstration.

## Sources (accessed 2026-11-02)
- **R1:** ReMA (Recycled Materials Association, formerly ISRI), "ReMA Releases Updates to Nonferrous Specs in the 2016 Scrap
  Specifications Circular": https://www.recycledmaterials.org/press-release/rema-releases-updates-to-nonferrous-specs-in-the-2016-scrap-specifications-circular/
  Twang-IAW: "Insulated aluminum wire… with the expected aluminum recovery agreed upon by the buyer and the seller. The material
  to be free of other wires and cables unless mutually agreed upon."
- **R2:** ReMA, "ReMA Scrap Specifications Circular Updated to Include New and Revised Nonferrous Specifications" (board approval
  2022-07-14): https://www.recycledmaterials.org/press-release/rema-scrap-specifications-circular-updated-to-include-new-and-revised-nonferrous-specifications/
  Aluminium wire and cable grades carry composition requirements (for example "Must contain 99.45% aluminum content"). Clean-metal grades are defined
  separately from insulated material.
- **R3:** USGS, *Mineral Commodity Summaries 2025*: Copper (https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2025/mcs2025-copper.pdf) and
  Aluminum (https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2025/mcs2025-aluminum.pdf). Market prices are quoted per pound and differ
  widely by metal (2024: copper COMEX about 420 ¢/lb, aluminium ingot about 130 ¢/lb). Numbers are used only as evidence; none are shown on screen.
- **R4:** WHO, "Electronic waste (e-waste)" fact sheet, 1 Oct 2024: https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/electronic-waste-(e-waste)
  "Open burning and heating are considered the most hazardous activities due to the toxic fumes created."
- **R5:** Hull City Council, "Cable burning": https://www.hull.gov.uk/environmental-crime-pollution/cable-burning. Burning cable to
  remove insulation "is a crime" (UK Clean Air Act 1993, s.33); "Dioxins, heavy metal and other forms of toxic pollutants" harm health.
