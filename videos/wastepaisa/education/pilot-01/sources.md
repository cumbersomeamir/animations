# Sources and claim map: pilot 01 "Why a paper cup is not just paper"

All sources are public, accessed **2026-11-02** from the Claude Code session. Quotes are short
excerpts used to support specific claims. No private data was used.

## Sources
| ID | Source | Date | What it supports |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | NIST news release, "NIST Study Shows Everyday Plastic Products Release Trillions of Microscopic Particles Into Water". https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2022/04/nist-study-shows-everyday-plastic-products-release-trillions-microscopic | Published 2022-04-20, updated 2025-02-03 | The single-use beverage cups studied "were coated with low-density polyethylene (LDPE)", "a soft flexible plastic film often used as a liner" |
| S2 | Grundon (UK waste management company), "Paper cup recycling" service page. https://www.grundon.com/services/paper-cup-recycling/ | Undated (© 2026) | Paper cups have "a polyethylene film bonded to them", which "makes the cup waterproof". "It is difficult for traditional recycling facilities to separate the paper from the polyethylene film". "It is the bonding between these two materials that makes it difficult to separate the paper fibres". Only a few facilities can process them |
| S3 | Wikipedia, "Paper cup". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paper_cup | Accessed revision 2026-11-02 | Cups are "often lined or coated with plastic" or wax to stop liquid "leaking out or soaking through the paper". | S4 | Waste Dive, K. | S6 | UK House of Commons Environmental Audit Committee, *Disposable Packaging: Coffee Cups* (HC 657, 2017–19) | Published Jan 2018 | **Not verified first-hand**: the primary page returned HTTP 403.
