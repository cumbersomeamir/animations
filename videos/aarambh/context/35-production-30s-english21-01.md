## Claim map (documented, interpretive, illustrative)
| claim | kind | basis |
|---|---|---|
| Blue babies lacked oxygen | documented | PBS: "the Tetralogy of Fallot ('blue baby syndrome')"; Taussig "suspected that the lack of oxygen resulted from a blockage in the blood vessels to the lungs or a leak in the septum". |
| Vivien Thomas helped fix it | documented | PBS: "Working with cardiologist Helen Taussig, Blalock and Thomas developed an operation that would deliver more oxygen to the blood"; JMB: he "played an important role in assisting Alfred Blalock and Helen Taussig in the development of the 'Blalock-Taussig' shunt". |
| then went uncredited | documented | JAMA: "the authors of this classic article did not acknowledge a key individual in the work… The silent partner was Vivien Thomas, an African American man and Blalock's surgical technician". |
| Too little of their blood reached the lungs | documented (simplified) | PBS (blockage in the vessels to the lungs); SP lists "inadequate pulmonary blood flow" among cyanotic lesions and says the shunt "improves blood flow to the lungs, reduces cyanosis". The septal leak (VSD) is not voiced. |
| A shunt rerouted an artery to the lungs, so more blood picked up oxygen | documented | SP: in the classical shunt "the subclavian artery was divided and anastomosed to the pulmonary artery"; the shunt "improves blood flow to the lungs, reduces cyanosis"; PBS: an operation "that would deliver more oxygen to the blood". |
