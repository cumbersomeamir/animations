## Topic-line correction (limits of her evidence)
- **The title line overclaims.** "Inge Lehmann heard a solid core" is not what her evidence showed.
  - Hjortenberg (2009): "it is not correct to say that she discovered the solid inner core, her model does not consider the question about whether the inner core is solid or fluid, that was done by others much later… Hence she discovered the inner core, that later proved to be solid."
  - So the narration says she proposed an inner core where waves speed up, and, separately, "Later evidence showed it is solid" (Dziewonski & Gilbert 1971; earlier proposals by Birch and Bullen per Hjortenberg).
- **"Heard" is figurative.** She read seismograms (recordings of ground motion). It is kept only in the brief's frame-0 words. "The impossible" is truthful in the sense of her own 1987 account: under a two-shell Earth "we could not have observations recorded between 102°… and 143°".

## Ray physics (illustration)
- **Engine:** `src/earth.mjs` traces rays in the cut plane through a simplified PREM-like P-velocity profile. It conserves the ray parameter p = r·sin(i)/v (Snell's law for a layered sphere) and mirrors the downgoing half about the deepest point.
- **Checks against the sources:**
  - the direct-P limit is about 95° (geometric, no diffraction);
  - the PKP caustic (smallest PKP distance) is at 143.4°, matching L87's 143°;
  - the PKP ray (p = 240 s/rad) lands at 150.8°, with a travel time of about 20 min;
  - the PKiKP ray (p = 113.86 s/rad) reflects at 1221.5 km and lands at 125.2°, with a travel time of about 19 min.
- **On screen:**
  - the heads move with the computed travel time, so they visibly slow in the outer core;
  - the band uses L87's 102°–143°.
