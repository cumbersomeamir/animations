Alice Ball: her method, his name

At 23, Alice Ball made a leprosy oil usable, then lost the credit. Swallowed, it caused nausea; injected, it hurt and barely absorbed. She turned its fatty acids into ethyl esters: thin oils the body absorbs. She died at 24; her supervisor published the work without crediting her. A 1922 paper called it Ball's Method.

Lesson: a remedy only helps if patients can take it.
