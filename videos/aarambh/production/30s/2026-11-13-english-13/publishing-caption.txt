Elisha Otis: cut the cable

Elisha Otis rose on a platform, then had its cable cut. It held. Hoists of his day had a flaw: if the rope broke, the load fell. His brake: rope tension held two catches back; if it snapped, a spring threw them into toothed rails. He didn't invent the elevator; in 1854, he proved its safety brake in public.

Lesson: a new invention may need a test people can see, before anyone wants it.
