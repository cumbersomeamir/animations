How a burr became a fastener: George de Mestral and the hook-and-loop idea.

In 1941, burdock burrs stuck to Swiss engineer George de Mestral's clothes and his dog's fur. Under a microscope he saw why: tiny hooks catching looped threads. Working with a weaver in France for nearly eight years, he copied that structure: one strip of hooks, one of loops. Press them together and they grip; pull, and the flexible nylon hooks let go. His Swiss patent was filed in 1951 and granted in 1954.

Takeaway: study how nature solves a problem, then test the mechanism.
