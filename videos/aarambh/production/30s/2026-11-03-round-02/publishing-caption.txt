Hedy Lamarr and George Antheil: how a piano-roll idea took on radio jamming.

In 1942, actress Hedy Lamarr and composer George Antheil received U.S. Patent 2,292,387 for a "secret communication system". A torpedo guided on one fixed radio frequency could be jammed, so their design changed frequency again and again. Matching perforated rolls, like player-piano rolls, switched the sender and receiver together (the patent describes up to 88 frequencies). The U.S. Navy rejected it as too cumbersome, but frequency hopping later became a common part of wireless communication systems.
