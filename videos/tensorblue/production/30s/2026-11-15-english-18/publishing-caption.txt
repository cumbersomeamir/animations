Ten seconds in a noisy cafe, and Shazam names the song.

The trick? Turn sound into stars. Shazam's 2003 paper keeps only the loudest frequency peaks. Pairs of peaks become hashes: two frequencies and the gap between. Look them up, and noise makes random hits, but in the right recording, the hits share one time offset.

So it matches that exact recording. Humming needs a melody-based search.

We built a small version of the method from the paper (our code, not Shazam's) and tested it on made-up songs. A 10-second clip buried in cafe noise as loud as the music found the right song: 21 hits lined up at 37 seconds in, against at most 4 for any other song. A hummed version of the same tune found no match.
