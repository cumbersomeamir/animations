Ten thousand posts. Not ten thousand rows. So where do new posts come from?

Lists built for this only create the rows on screen, plus spares. A row that leaves the top isn't destroyed. It's reused for the next post. In our test: a plain list, ten thousand rows. A recycling list, fourteen. Android says it improves responsiveness and saves power.

A long feed is just a small loop of rows, refilled as you scroll.

Our test ran in Chromium on a phone-sized page (390×844) with 10,000 made-up posts: the plain list built one row per post; our recycling list kept 14 rows (12 on screen plus a spare at each end) and refilled them all the way to post 10,000.
