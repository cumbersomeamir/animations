Your video went blurry instead of freezing. The app chose that.

But how does it switch mid-video? Streaming apps cut video into short chunks, each saved in several qualities. The player picks each next chunk by how full its buffer is. Weak signal drains the buffer, so it grabs smaller chunks.

In our test, always-HD froze for nearly twelve seconds. Adaptive never froze. So a blurry moment means the app chose to keep playing.

Our test is a simulation we wrote (not any app's code): 4-second chunks, a made-up Wi-Fi signal that drops from 8 to 1.2 Mb/s for 26 seconds, and a buffer-based player like the one described in a Stanford and Netflix study. The always-HD player froze for 11.6 seconds; the adaptive one kept playing by switching to lower qualities. With a smaller safety margin the adaptive player can still freeze briefly, so adapting reduces freezing rather than guaranteeing zero.
