JUST PLAYED. UP AGAIN? Why Spotify made shuffle "less random", without breaking the randomness.

Spotify's shuffle was truly random. It felt repetitive. Five heads in a row is random too. So what changed? Since 2025, it makes several random orders. Each gets a freshness score. Songs you just played lose points near the top. The freshest order wins.

Our test: just-played songs came first one time in four. Freshest of ten: one in twelve. Building a feed? Keep it random, then pick the freshest of a few.

Our test (our own Python model of the method Spotify described, not Spotify's code; Spotify hasn't published how many orders it draws or its exact scoring): a 20-song playlist where 5 songs were just played, 20,000 shuffles. A pure shuffle opened with a just-played song 24.6% of the time. Drawing 10 random orders and keeping the one with the fewest recent songs near the top cut that to 8.1%. The candidate counts and weights are our own choices.

Notes: "Fewer Repeats" is the default for Spotify Premium users, and Standard Shuffle stays purely random. Each candidate order is a normal random shuffle; only the pick between them is guided by freshness. The queues, songs, coins, cards and die in the video are our own illustration, not Spotify's app.
