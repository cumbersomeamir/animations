It raced to ninety-nine percent. Now it's stuck. Why?

The bar only counted the download. But an update still verifies the file, unpacks thousands of pieces, and installs them. A bar shouldn't say a hundred until it's all done, so it waits.

Our test hit ninety-nine in three seconds, then sat there almost two more. Microsoft's design guide calls that particularly uninformative and annoying. Building one? Give every stage its share of the bar.

Our test was a real four-stage update on one computer (not any app's installer): download a 37.7 MB package at 100 Mb/s from a local server, check its SHA-256, unzip 2,401 files, and install each file safely to disk. A bar that counted only downloaded bytes reached 99% at 3.0 s and stayed there for 1.8 s, 37% of the wait. A bar where every stage had a share sized from a previous run was never more than 6 points away from the time actually elapsed (the bytes-only bar was up to 36 points away). Timings are from this one machine.
