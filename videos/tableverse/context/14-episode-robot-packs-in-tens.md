## Facts (official CCSS Mathematics PDF, https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Math_Standards1.pdf,
sha256 2d28ded26c7394f55525550e32bb96786da2e9a3276ccca8873e80ebcdebab11, retrieved 2026-11-13, re-read 2026-11-15)
- 3.NBT.A.3: "Multiply one-digit whole numbers by multiples of 10 in the range 10–90 (e.g., 9 × 80, 5 × 60) using
  strategies based on place value and properties of operations." → 4 × 30.
- 2.NBT.A.1a: "100 can be thought of as a bundle of ten tens — called a 'hundred.'" → the hundred crate.
- 3.OA.B.5 (associative example): "3 × 5 × 2 can be found by 3 × 5 = 15, then 15 × 2 = 30, or by 5 × 2 = 10, then
  3 × 10 = 30." → 4 × 30 = 4 × (3 × 10) = (4 × 3) × 10 = 12 tens.
- 3.OA.A.1 (equal groups): "interpret 5 × 7 as the total number of objects in 5 groups of 7 objects each."
- Measured facts = arithmetic: 4 × 30 = 120 = 12 × 10 = 100 + 20; 4 × 3 = 12. Counted objects on screen: exactly
  120 cookies (4 rows × 2 lines × 15) → 12 tubes × 10 slices. The bakery, robot, tubes and crate are illustrative;
  "twice as fast" is not claimed (the robot simply packs rows 2–4 quickly). No app capability claimed.
