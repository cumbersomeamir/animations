Decision: the proposal (five carriages × three little pillows = 15) is plain equal groups — feedback-01's topic, and batch
22 also counted equal groups (4, 8, 12). As instructed, an unrelated subject inside the genre was chosen rather than
another equal-groups version: the five times table on a clock (measuring a time interval in five-minute jumps). It
differs from batch 22 on all three required axes — core question ("Is it time yet?" vs "Why is there never room?"),
setting (an enchanted cloud station with a big station clock vs a moon-hotel lobby with lifts) and mechanism (reading the
big hand: each number = 5 minutes, a time interval vs grouping into containers with a remainder). No earlier episode used
a clock. Kept from the brief: the original sleepy dragon, the cloud train, the absurd giant pillow (it jams in the train
door and finally pops through), the frame-zero picture of the dragon squeezed under one huge pillow, funny moving
characters, the fact 15 = 5 × 3 delivered only at the payoff (shown as 3 × 5 = 15 to match "three jumps of five" in the
narration — the CCSS reading "3 groups of 5"), and the TableVerse CTA. The frame-0 words became "IS IT TIME YET?" (the
story's real question); "THE PILLOW IS TOO BIG!" stays as the pillow gag on screen.

## Entertainment plan (feedback applied)
A dragon pinned under a pillow three times its size, snoring (paper "z"s); peeks one eye open and yawns; flops back; the
station clock ticks on every beat and the big hand springs forward five minutes at a time; at ten minutes the dragon
charges the door too early with the pillow overhead — it jams (boing, stars) and bounces back; the approved robot,
now the station conductor in a cap, wags a finger, then blows the whistle; at "All aboard!" the pillow squeezes through
the door like toothpaste and pops inside (cork pop); the dragon hops in and waves from a window while the pillow bulges
out of another; the train puffs away on the cloud rails. Hard cuts on the 100 bpm beat grid between dragon close-ups,
clock close-ups and the station; blurred cloud puffs as the nearest depth layer; the music drops out for the snore.
