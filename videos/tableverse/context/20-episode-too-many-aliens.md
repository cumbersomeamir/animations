Decision: the brief's maths — three lifts with four aliens each, 3 × 4 = 12 as equal groups — is exactly what
feedback-01 taught in its second half ("three groups of four still make twelve"), so by this app's rule it is covered.
The replacement keeps almost everything the brief asked for: the moon hotel, three lifts with four seats each, the
impatient tiny alien squashed against an absurdly full lift, "TOO MANY ALIENS!" on frame 0, 12 aliens grouped and
counted once, 3 × 4 = 12 delivered only at the payoff. The one change: the tiny alien is the thirteenth, so every lift
really is full — and the useful new idea is the leftover (remainder): 12 seats for 13 aliens leaves exactly one over.
That idea was never taught before, makes "TOO MANY ALIENS!" literally true, and turns the brief's squeezing gag into
the question the story answers ("Why is there never room?").

## Entertainment plan (feedback applied)
Rule-of-three slapstick: squeezed in lift 1 (squished faces, doors straining, pop!), lift 2 (belly-bounce, pop!),
lift 3 (doors catch its antenna — it stretches like elastic and twangs free, lands on its bottom, dizzy). Impatience:
stomping, arms crossed, foot taps on the beat, steam from its antenna. Musical cues: the three lift dings are tuned to an
A-flat chord (rising), the score thins to the bass while it taps, chimes for the payoff. Reveal: all three lifts open,
the twelve wave; the dashed group outlines build one per count; everyone turns to look at number thirteen in a pool of
light; payoff sign; a dashed "left over" ring. Funny resolution: the twelve ride up, lift 1 comes back empty and the
tiny alien zooms in and sprawls across all the seats, then winks. Hard cuts on the beat (100 bpm, 18 frames) between
close-ups and wide shots; blurred moon rocks as the nearest depth layer.
