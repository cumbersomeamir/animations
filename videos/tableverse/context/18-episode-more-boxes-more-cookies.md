Decision: "8 × 3 as twice 4 × 3" is the doubling strategy english15-01 already taught ("for a six fact, find the three
fact, then double it") — the same move the app's history already rejected for batch 17 ("6 × 4 = 3 × 4 doubled").
Kept everything else the brief asked for: the cookie-shop mouse (approved english-16/19 puppet), boxes that double from
4 into 8, the 8 × 3 fact, funny visual action, entertainment first and a correct 24-cookie payoff — and taught the one
not-yet-covered idea that picture holds: the mouse doubles the boxes by sawing every box in half, so each box holds
half as many and the total stays 24 (8 × 3 = 4 × 6, "double and halve"). The comedy is the misconception itself: the
greedy mouse believes more boxes means more cookies; the count proves the same 24.

## Facts (sources read 2026-11-18)
- CCSS Mathematics (official PDF https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Math_Standards1.pdf,
  sha256 2d28ded26c7394f55525550e32bb96786da2e9a3276ccca8873e80ebcdebab11):
  - 3.OA.A.1 "Interpret products of whole numbers, e.g., interpret 5 × 7 as the total number of objects in 5 groups of
    7 objects each." → 4 boxes of 6 = 4 × 6; 8 boxes of 3 = 8 × 3.
  - 3.OA.B.5 "Apply properties of operations as strategies to multiply and divide. … 3 × 5 × 2 can be found by
    3 × 5 = 15, then 15 × 2 = 30, or by 5 × 2 = 10, then 3 × 10 = 30. (Associative property of multiplication.)"
    → 8 × 3 = (4 × 2) × 3 = 4 × (2 × 3) = 4 × 6 = 24.
  - 3.OA.C.7 "Fluently multiply and divide within 100, using strategies such as … properties of operations."
